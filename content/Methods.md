# Methods

This chapter documents how the review was produced. Every figure, rate and count
below is read from a pipeline artifact — the ten cluster evidence files, the
twelve per-phase gate JSONs, the phase ledger, and the frame ledger for project
`proj_9238b857f2b8`. Values the pipeline did not record are marked as not recorded
rather than estimated. The language model that executed every agent frame was
`claude-opus-5`, run under the Expert Review Pipeline v29 protocol.

(sec-methods-search)=
## Search Strategy

Searches were organised around the ten evidence clusters defined in `gate_scope.json`,
each feeding one or more body sections. Retrieval ran entirely against public REST
endpoints called from Python's standard library, because the `requests` and `certifi`
packages were absent from the execution environment and package installation was
unavailable; HTTPS therefore used an unverified SSL context. The endpoints recorded in
`gate_evidence_compliance.json` are:

- Europe PMC REST search, `https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search` with `resultType=core` — the primary discovery endpoint for every cluster
- Europe PMC full-text XML, `https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/{PMCID}/fullTextXML`
- NCBI E-utilities, `https://eutils.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/entrez/eutils/efetch.fcgi?db=pmc`
- CrossRef, `https://api.crossref.org/works/{doi}`, for bibliographic metadata
- an open-access full-text fetch helper backed by Unpaywall and PMC

Coverage was therefore Europe PMC-dominant. No contact email was available to the
pipeline, so CrossRef and NCBI were called without a `mailto` parameter.

|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|

Deduplication across clusters reduced 836 reviewed papers to
**726 unique DOIs**, the figure certified by the Phase-3 citation

clusters recorded query strings in their evidence file; five

`evidence_parameters.snowball_rounds` was **0**: no forward or backward citation chasing was
performed in either direction. Cluster C07's evidence file records `snowball_rounds_run: 0`

(sec-methods-inclusion)=
## Inclusion and Exclusion Criteria

The scope record fixed these evidence parameters:

- `min_papers_per_cluster`: **70** (a floor, not a target)
- `snowball_rounds`: **0**
- `saturation_criterion`: not set
- `total_bibliography_target`: not set; `bibliography_target_is_hard_floor`: true

Every cluster cleared the floor, with per-cluster paper counts from
77 to
108. Inclusion required that a record be
primary research within the cluster's declared scope and that the sentence supporting each
extracted claim be present verbatim in the retrieved text. Peripheral endocrine and oncology
records were excluded at screening in the clusters whose screening rule is recorded.
Preprint–journal duplicate pairs were resolved in favour of the published version.

Nineteen search-failure records are logged across the ten cluster packages. Their
dispositions, counted from the `search_failures` arrays, are:

| Disposition | Records |
|---|---:|
| Paper retained with `text_access = abstract_only` after full text could not be obtained | 6 |
| Paper excluded and the shortfall logged as an evidence gap | 7 |
| Evidence gap logged with no paper added | 4 |
| Preprint excluded in favour of its published version | 1 |
| Previously extracted finding removed rather than re-worded | 1 |
| **Total** | **19** |

No paper was excluded solely because its full text was unavailable: where an abstract carried
the claim verbatim, the paper was retained and marked abstract-only. Papers for which neither
full text nor a usable abstract could be retrieved appear in the eleven evidence-gap records
above rather than in the findings.

(sec-methods-retrieval)=
## Full-Text Retrieval

Full-text retrieval was attempted for every candidate through Europe PMC `fullTextXML`, NCBI
E-utilities `efetch` against PMC, and an Unpaywall/PMC-backed fetch helper. **The publisher and
aggregator APIs named in the pipeline template were not all used: no Elsevier, Springer Nature,
OpenAlex or NCBI API key was available in this environment, so those sources were not queried.**
Retrieval rates per source were not recorded separately; only the resulting per-finding
`text_access` label is.

Of 844 findings, **461 are sourced from a
retrieved article body and 383 from an abstract**, an overall
full-text share of **54.6%**. The share varies sharply by
cluster, from **100% in C07** (circuit consequences) to **25% in C08** (human postmortem,
single-nucleus and CSF evidence); C02 (32%) and C04 (31%) are similarly constrained. The
shortfall is concentrated in pre-2000 closed-access postmortem and CSF literature, which PMC
holds only as scanned records: the recorded failure modes are PMC returning a sub-15 KB record
with no `<body>` element, HTTP 500 from `fullTextXML`, and DOIs with no PMCID at all.

A 15 KB size-validation threshold is recorded in the evidence files for five clusters
(C01, C02, C04, C07, C10): a retrieval below that size, or lacking a `<body>` element, was
treated as a failed full-text fetch and the paper was marked abstract-only. The remaining five
clusters do not record whether the threshold was applied. Where a PDF was the only available
form, text was recovered by inflating the PDF content streams with `zlib` and extracting
literal strings, because no PDF library could be installed.

Source-sentence validation was run per cluster: each claim sentence was asserted to be a
verbatim substring of the Europe PMC abstract (for abstract-only findings) or of the retrieved
article body (for full-text findings), with full-text sentences additionally asserted absent
from the abstract.

## Evidence Extraction

Each cluster produced an evidence file in a fixed schema. Every finding carries `doi`,
`cite_key`, `claim`, `claim_source_sentence`, `evidence`, `effect_size`,
`effect_size_source_sentence`, `n`, `study_system`, `replication_status`,
`replication_evidence_dois` and `text_access`. Packages additionally carry `conflicts`,
`unreplicated_claims`, `evidence_gaps`, `figure_data`, `search_failures` and
`source_sentence_validation`.

| Metric | Value |
|---|---:|
| Findings extracted | 844 |
| Unique DOIs | 726 |
| Conflicts recorded | 90 |
| Figure-data comparisons | 52 (spanning 190 papers) |
| Findings with an effect size | 805 (95.4%) |
| Findings with a sample size | 220 (26.1%) |
| Strict schema compliance | 99.526% (840/844) |

The gap between the effect-size rate (95.4%) and the
sample-size rate (26.1%) reflects the source literature:
abstracts frequently state a magnitude without a cohort size, and the pipeline recorded `n` only
where it was stated in the retrieved text.

Replication status across the 844 findings:

| Status | Findings | Share |
|---|---:|---:|
| Independently replicated | 261 | 30.9% |
| Replication unknown | 451 | 53.4% |
| Contested | 132 | 15.6% |

Phase 5 reorganised the cluster packages into twelve per-section packages organised by
argument, carrying 809 distinct DOI–sentence
records; 64 DOIs appear in more than one section, and in no case does the same DOI contribute
the same source sentence to two sections.

Four field-level corrections were applied to evidence files after their gates had passed.
Each is logged in the affected package's own `corrections` array rather than applied silently:

- **Davies1990** (`evidence_section_09.json`) — the `claim` field had extended a fibre-length null result to perikaryal counts; corrected in the section package, with the certified cluster file left unmodified because the defect was introduced downstream.
- **Ouellet2014** (`evidence_section_01.json`) — `study_system` and `claim` corrected against the CrossRef title for `10.3389/fnana.2014.00040`; the study is rat primary auditory cortex, not mouse cortex. The correction aligns this package with the section 08 package for the same DOI.
- **Frankowski2022** (`evidence_section_01.json`) — supplementary source sentences added, traced to the section 07 package for the same DOI.
- **Hofling2019** (`evidence_section_12.json`) — a reported range traced to the section 04 package's source sentence for the same DOI.

(sec-methods-citation-verification)=
## Citation Verification

Four mechanical verification passes ran, all against live databases and none of them sampled.

**Phase 3 — citation infrastructure.** All 726 unique DOIs were
re-queried against `api.crossref.org/works/{doi}`; 726 resolved.
Cite keys were derived mechanically from the CrossRef first-author surname and publication year,
giving zero orphan DOIs and zero unused keys.

**Phase 9 — bibliography.** The 726-entry `references.bib` was checked
against a fresh CrossRef fetch of all 726 DOIs on fourteen named
checks, all of which passed: DOI resolution, first-author surname match, title similarity
(threshold 0.85, minimum observed 0.875), exact year match, journal match for the 700 journal
articles, volume/issue/pages match, pure-ASCII encoding, key uniqueness, key derivation, and
contamination screening. Across the ten body sections, **1441
citation occurrences resolve to 726 distinct keys**, with
0 unresolved keys and 0
uncited bibliography entries.

**Phase 15 — citation triples.** Extraction was exhaustive rather than sampled: one triple per
individual cite key, **1,534 in total**, each pairing a key with the sentence that carries it and
with the bibliography record behind it. The count was checked against the document two
independent ways — by counting cite keys across all `{cite:p}` and `{cite:t}` directives and by
counting the extracted triples — and the two agree.

**Phase 16 — claim-level verification.** Every triple was adjudicated against the cited paper's
own text. The adjudication returned **1,351 VERIFIED, 122 MINOR, 25 MISATTRIBUTED, 0 CHIMERIC and
0 HALLUCINATED**, with a further **36 records unverifiable because the source text could not be
retrieved**. No citation in this review resolves to a non-existent paper, and none resolves to a
different paper than the one named.

**Phases 17 and 18 — fix preparation and execution.** The 147 defect records were consolidated
into **118 fix requests**, of which **113 were applied, 5 were not applied and 0 failed**. Four of
the five unapplied requests were conditional deletions: each proposed removing a value as
unsourceable, and in each case the value turned out to be carried by a source sentence in the
section's evidence file, so the value was kept rather than deleted. The fifth proposed deleting a
duplicate bibliography entry; it was declined because the entry is still cited three times in the
Alzheimer's disease section, and removing it would have left those citations unresolved.

**Final state.** The bibliography holds **726 entries against 726 distinct cite keys, with zero
unresolved keys and zero uncited entries in both the MyST content and the LaTeX manuscript**, and
the file is pure ASCII.

**What this does not establish.** The 36 unverifiable records are a limit of coverage, not a clean
result. They are claims whose source text could not be retrieved — concentrated in the
closed-access literature that this review reads at abstract level — and they remain unchecked
rather than checked and passed. The verified share above is a share of the 1,498 triples that
could be checked, and the honest reading of the remainder is that nobody looked.

## Prose Standardisation

The twelve body sections were drafted in parallel by independent agents, which left them
differing in sentence construction. A single post-processing pass, run after the review was
first published, restyled the prose of all twelve against one written standard
(`comprev-writing-style`, pipeline generation 30). **Nothing about the evidence, the claims, the
citations or the numbers was revisited. Only the wording changed.**

**Method.** Each section file was split mechanically into prose segments and protected segments.
Protected segments are headings, anchors, figure directives, dropdowns, admonitions and embedded
figure code. Only prose was sent to a restyling agent, one agent per section. Each file was then
reassembled from the returned prose and its untouched protected segments. The splitter was
verified to reproduce every file byte-for-byte before use, so each embedded figure-generating
code block in the published document is identical to the one that produced the figure.

**What the standard requires:**

- one finding per sentence
- enumerations of three or more parallel items set as lists, not as a comma series inside a sentence
- a sentence ending on its finding rather than on its citation
- paragraphs opening on a claim rather than on a study
- a claim stated before the doubt about it
- subjects that name agents, and verbs that carry the action
- one typographic convention for dispersion, minus signs, ranges and parenthetical dashes

**Measured effect**, counting each list item as a sentence. Mean sentence length fell from 44.6
words to roughly 20 across the twelve sections. The longest sentence in the review fell from 188
words to under 60. Comma-series enumerations were converted to lists throughout. Word count was
unchanged, because the material moved into lists rather than being cut.

**Fidelity.** Every citation role, numeric value and cross-reference was compared between the
pre-restyle and post-restyle text of each section, mechanically and in full. Citation roles were
compared as ordered key tuples, so a multi-key role could not be split or merged. Numeric
comparison normalised hyphen, en dash and minus sign, because the typographic rule converts
hyphens to true minus signs in negative numbers and to en dashes in ranges. **Across all twelve
sections the three sets are identical: no citation key, numeric value or cross-reference was
added, dropped or altered.** Anchors, headings, directive counts and figure paths were confirmed
identical at whole-file level.

**Two limits of this pass, stated plainly.**

First, **claim-level citation verification was performed on the pre-restyle text.** The 1,534
triples adjudicated in Phase 16 pair each citation with the sentence that carried it *before*
this pass. The restyle preserved every key and every number mechanically, and no claim was
knowingly altered. But the sentences those verdicts were issued against are not in every case the
sentences now published, and the verification was not re-run afterwards.

Second, the standard forbids body prose from describing how this review retrieved its material,
on the grounds that such a statement describes the review rather than the published record.
Twelve such passages were rewritten to hedge the claim instead of narrating the search. Two
specifics those passages carried are recorded here instead. Retrieval was dominated by one
full-text index with no citation chasing, and no claim of complete coverage is made. Of the
abstract-only records behind the open-questions section, ten were published from 2018 onward, so
the full-text shortfall is not confined to older closed-access work. Per-cluster full-text
coverage is reported above in {ref}`sec-methods-retrieval`.
