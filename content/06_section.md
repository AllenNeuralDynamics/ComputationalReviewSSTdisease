(sec-huntingtons)=

# Huntington's Disease and Striatal SST Interneurons

Dopamine depletion leaves the striatal peptidergic system altered but alive, as set out in {ref}`sec-parkinsons-lewy-body`; Huntington's disease (HD) poses the complementary question: what becomes of this interneuron class when its postsynaptic targets are destroyed around it.

(sec-06-sparing-claim)=

## The sparing claim and its measurement basis

The textbook answer is that it is spared. That answer traces to a specific and narrow measurement base. Postmortem HD striatum contains two- to threefold higher somatostatin and NPY concentrations than control tissue, an increase the original reports attributed partly to striatal atrophy {cite:p}`Beal1991b`; peptide increases of similar size in mild and severe neuropathological grades were read by those authors as consistent with a relative but not absolute sparing of the aspiny neurons in which somatostatin and NPY are colocalised {cite:p}`Beal1988`. Elevated NPY in cerebrospinal fluid is measured directly in HD cohorts {cite:p}`Wagner2016`, while the higher NPY-positive neuron counts in the HD caudate-putamen reach this literature as a restatement inside a later spatial-transcriptomic study rather than as a primary count retrieved here {cite:p}`Burns2025`, and in primate excitotoxin models GABA and substance P immunoreactivity fall while somatostatin-like, NPY-like and choline acetyltransferase immunoreactivity show no significant depletion {cite:p}`Ferrante1993`. The canonical syntheses state the position compactly: spiny projection neurons and parvalbumin interneurons degenerate progressively while cholinergic and nitric oxide synthase (NOS)-containing interneurons are relatively spared {cite:p}`Morigaki2017`, the NADPH-diaphorase-positive cells reportedly even at late stages {cite:p}`Mitchell1999`.

Three features of that base deserve stating plainly. First, peptide concentration is normalised to tissue that is itself shrinking, so an unchanged number of peptide-containing cells in a smaller striatum yields a concentration increase — which the report of the two- to threefold rise named as a possible cause {cite:p}`Beal1991b`, while the companion grade analysis drew the relative-sparing reading from the similarity of the increases across grades {cite:p}`Beal1988`. Second, the histochemical arm of the claim rests on NADPH-diaphorase staining {cite:p}`Brouillet1995, Beal1993`, a readout that reports the presence of the marker and therefore cannot by itself separate surviving cells from functionally intact ones. Third, and most consequentially, a primary demonstration of absolute sparing using modern cell-type-specific markers with unbiased stereology was not retrieved by these searches; the quantitative counts that do exist come from mouse models and do not agree with each other, one reporting small marker-positive reductions {cite:p}`Rajput2011` and the other no significant change in striatal somatostatin interneuron number {cite:p}`Rallapalle2021`. The claim that striatal interneurons are spared in HD is therefore repeated far more confidently than its primary data support, and the excitotoxicity literature on which it was originally built, treated in {ref}`sec-selective-vulnerability`, was never designed to settle cell number. Forty of the eighty reports underlying this section were available as full text; the 1980s and 1990s peptide and radioimmunoassay series carrying much of the sparing claim is represented here by abstracts only, an asymmetry that falls precisely on the historical foundation of the argument.

(sec-06-peptide-transcript)=

## Peptide up, transcript down: the dissociation inside the spared population

The sharpest internal evidence against reading sparing as integrity comes from putting peptide and transcript measurements side by side in the same region. In situ hybridisation in human HD brain found neuronal nitric oxide synthase mRNA significantly decreased in caudate nucleus and putamen but not in sensorimotor cortex, with the decrease becoming more pronounced as neuropathological severity increased {cite:p}`Norris1996`. That is the same striatum in which somatostatin and NPY peptide concentrations rise {cite:p}`Beal1988, Beal1991b`. Transcript loss that tracks severity inside a population whose peptide content rises is what one expects of surviving cells with downregulated transcription in atrophic tissue, not of a population unaffected by the mutation. The same picture appears in other molecular readouts of this class: cannabinoid CB1 receptor expression is downregulated in striatal NPY/nNOS-expressing interneurons in R6/2 mice, in Hdh(Q150/Q150) mice and in human HD caudate {cite:p}`Horne2012`, cannabinoid receptor loss in R6/1 mice is concentrated in hippocampal GABAergic interneurons, where it is the likely cause of impaired depolarisation-induced suppression of inhibition in a different interneuron population from the striatal class at issue here {cite:p}`DiFranco2025`, cholinergic interneurons and astroglia in HD knock-in mice downregulate a large set of projection-neuron marker genes {cite:p}`Lee2020`, and surveys of striatal nuclei report bidirectional dysregulation of cell-type-specific gene networks, with altered signalling probability for the NPY/SST interneuron cluster itself {cite:p}`Malaiya2021, Jiang2024`. The receptor system through which somatostatin signalling might protect these cells is itself coupled to that remodelling: co-activation of somatostatin receptors with CB1 abolishes the protection that somatostatin receptor activation confers in huntingtin knock-in striatal cells {cite:p}`Zou2019`.

:::{admonition} Evidence Conflict
:class: warning
**Peptide concentration versus transcript level in the same region.** Radioimmunoassay of HD striatum reports increased somatostatin and NPY, read as relative sparing of the cells that contain them {cite:p}`Beal1988`; in situ hybridisation of the same region reports significantly decreased nNOS mRNA that worsens with grade {cite:p}`Norris1996`. The disagreement is not about which result is correct but about what each measures — peptide content normalised to atrophic tissue versus per-neuron transcript — and it is resolved by that distinction rather than by new data. Both are compatible with surviving but transcriptionally downregulated cells, and neither establishes functional integrity.
:::

The excitotoxic provenance of the sparing claim is also less unanimous than its repetition suggests. Injection of N-methyl-D-aspartate (NMDA)-type agonists into rat dorsolateral frontoparietal cortex reduces glutamate and GABA markers while somatostatin, NPY, substance P, cholecystokinin and VIP are unchanged or increased {cite:p}`Beal1991a`, and the sparing is agonist-specific: kainate, quisqualate and AMPA produce dose-dependent reductions in all four markers examined, which is the basis of the inference that resistance reflects a paucity of NMDA receptors rather than general excitotoxic resistance {cite:p}`Beal1989`. In culture, striatal neurons containing either NADPH-diaphorase or acetylcholinesterase are more resistant to NMDA and quinolinate than the general striatal population, though the resistance is not absolute and is overcome by intense exposure; heightened vulnerability to kainate is confined to the NADPH-diaphorase-containing neurons and is not shown by the acetylcholinesterase-containing ones {cite:p}`Koh1988`. Against this, a stereological study of intrastriatal quinolinic acid lesions found parvalbumin- and NPY-positive interneuron numbers significantly reduced relative to control in the peri-lesional transition zone, reported as a test statistic without an effect-size magnitude {cite:p}`Feng2014`, and an earlier comparison, represented here only by its abstract, states that the preservation of somatostatin/NPY/NADPH-diaphorase-containing neurons found in HD was not observed following quinolinic acid lesions {cite:p}`Davies1988`. The one pharmacological test of the obvious mechanistic candidate retrieved here was negative: systemic NOS inhibition neither protected against nor potentiated quinolinic-acid-induced loss of NADPH-diaphorase, NOS or acetylcholinesterase neurons {cite:p}`Mackenzie1995`.

Candidate determinants of the differential outcome remain correlational and scattered across preparations. nNOS-positive striatal neurons show no detectable mitochondrial reactive oxygen species rise on NMDA receptor activation while nNOS-negative neurons do, although the same report records a further set of experiments in which no difference between nNOS-positive and nNOS-negative striatal neurons was observed {cite:p}`Canzoniero2013`; group I metabotropic glutamate receptor activation depolarises large aspiny cholinergic interneurons without affecting spiny neuron membrane properties {cite:p}`Calabresi1999`; and huntingtin protein itself is distributed unevenly, with little or no huntingtin immunoreactivity in choline acetyltransferase-, parvalbumin-, calretinin- or nNOS-positive interneurons in one survey {cite:p}`Kosinski1997` but concentration *in* striatal interneurons, and detectable huntingtin message in only about 65% of projection neurons, in another {cite:p}`Fusco1999`, against a background of topographically and cellularly heterogeneous huntingtin immunoreactivity in human neostriatum {cite:p}`Ferrante1997`. For three further mechanistic proposals in this literature, no independent replication was retrieved by these searches in the fourteen to thirty years since publication: that somatostatin itself is the feature conferring sparing under excitotoxicity {cite:p}`Kumar2008`, that optineurin is abundant in striatal interneurons and low in projection neurons in a pattern complementary to HD loss {cite:p}`Okita2012`, and that huntingtin-associated protein 1 mRNA is co-enriched with nNOS mRNA in discrete neuronal populations {cite:p}`Li1996a`. No study manipulating one of these candidates and measuring interneuron survival in a genetic HD model was retrieved by these searches, so the mechanism of the differential outcome is, at present, an inference from correlations gathered across decades and preparations.

(sec-06-parvalbumin-contrast)=

## Sparing relative to what: the parvalbumin contrast

Sparing is in any case a comparative statement, and the comparison class matters. Parvalbumin interneurons carry the most consistent loss evidence in the striatum: reduced per-area density in the dorsal striatum of 12–14-month BACHD mice where somatostatin-positive interneuron numbers in the same animals at the same stage are unchanged {cite:p}`Rallapalle2021`, major count reductions in the caudate nucleus of two transgenic HD macaques {cite:p}`Lallani2019`, markedly reduced density with somatic atrophy in R6/2 striatum {cite:p}`Paldino2017`, and significant reduction after excitotoxic lesion {cite:p}`Feng2014`. In cortex the picture divides rather than converges: severe loss in the human anterior cingulate cortex of mood-dominant HD cases {cite:p}`Kim2014` sits against unchanged parvalbumin density in R6/2 cortex and unchanged parvalbumin counts in BACHD cortex at the stage measured {cite:p}`Voelkl2022, Molero2026`, and cortical GABA-release deficits have been produced in one model without any loss of parvalbumin interneurons at all {cite:p}`Dougherty2014`. The clinical reading of that class — that parvalbumin interneuron loss may contribute to late dystonia and rigidity — is itself a proposal rather than a demonstration {cite:p}`Reiner2018`. {numref}`fig-sec6-sst-sparing-versus-pv-loss-hd` places the somatostatin and parvalbumin measures on the same page, and the exercise exposes how unlike each other the underlying measurements are: an area-normalised density, a percent marker reduction, a raw count on a study's own scale and a soma-size morphometric cannot be read as one series, and the parvalbumin evidence spans a mouse, a monkey, a human cortex and a rat lesion in three different units, with only the monkey and the human measures sharing a reporting convention.

Within the mouse literature the quantitative disagreement is small but real, and it lines up with construct. R6/2, an exon-1 fragment model, yields reported reductions of 14% and 8% in brain nitric oxide synthase- and somatostatin-positive striatal neurons {cite:p}`Rajput2011`, while BACHD, a full-length transgenic, yields no significant change in somatostatin-positive interneuron number in the same region {cite:p}`Rallapalle2021`. The two can be reconciled by model aggressiveness rather than by discarding either, and the same construct dimension organises much of the disagreement in this field, as {ref}`sec-conflicts-limitations` sets out. What neither study establishes is whether the quantity measured is a count of cells or of immunoreactive profiles — a distinction that is not a technicality here, because in R6/2 cortex reduced somatostatin and VIP immunoreactive density coexists with unchanged genetically labelled cell numbers for all interneuron types, which the authors read as loss of molecular marker in the absence of cell death {cite:p}`Voelkl2022`.

(sec-06-functional-pathology)=

## Functional pathology in a surviving population

If sparing means survival rather than integrity, the physiological measurements should show it, and they do — inconsistently. Optogenetic silencing of somatostatin-expressing interneurons with enhanced halorhodopsin reduced spontaneous inhibitory postsynaptic current (sIPSC) frequency in spiny projection neurons of Q175 knock-in mice but not of wild-types, an all-cell effect that the study's own summary attributes to a subset of those neurons {cite:p}`Holley2019`, implying that this class supplies a disproportionate share of projection-neuron inhibition in the HD striatum; the same laboratory had earlier attributed increased GABAergic input to striatal cholinergic interneurons in symptomatic R6/2 mice to somatostatin-expressing low-threshold-spiking rather than parvalbumin fast-spiking interneurons {cite:p}`Holley2015`. Paired recording with optogenetic activation reaches the opposite assignment: parvalbumin fast-spiking interneuron stimulation evokes significantly larger medium spiny neuron responses in HD mice while activation of somatostatin-expressing cells produces no genotype difference {cite:p}`Cepeda2013`. Baseline spontaneous currents complicate both: mean sIPSC current in BACHD striatal medium spiny neurons does not differ from wild type, though that value is a tabulated striatal measurement inside a study whose reported recordings are cortical and whose striatal recording age is not stated {cite:p}`Spampanato2008`, GABA-A receptor subunit expression is remodelled in a cell-type-specific direction, with α1 reduced on parvalbumin interneuron somata in R6/1 mice {cite:p}`Du2017`; parvalbumin terminal puncta on projection-neuron somata redistribute between striosome and matrix compartments, although the reported change is shared by HD and control non-learner animals rather than being genotype-specific {cite:p}`Friedman2020`; parvalbumin interneurons in Q175 mice are described as receiving fewer excitatory inputs at symptomatic stages, with a lowered excitation–inhibition ratio, though the quantitative form of that claim reaches this literature by secondary restatement rather than through a primary report retrieved here {cite:p}`Thabault2026`; and in a striatal network model a gradual reduction of effective inhibitory conductance onto projection neurons at advancing symptomatic ages is the form that progressive projection-neuron loss would take, in partial agreement with that model's results {cite:p}`Ponzi2020`, with failure of striatal chloride homeostasis a candidate substrate for such a change {cite:p}`Serranilla2022`. {numref}`fig-sec6-inhibition-onto-projection-neurons-hd` shows why the field's summary phrase "increased inhibition in HD" is assembled from quantities that cannot be added.

:::{admonition} Evidence Conflict
:class: warning
**Which interneuron class carries the excess inhibition onto projection neurons.** Silencing somatostatin-expressing interneurons changes projection-neuron sIPSC frequency far more in Q175 than in wild type {cite:p}`Holley2019`; paired recording and optogenetic activation of the same class in R6/2 and knock-in mice find no genotype difference in the responses that class evokes and locate the clearest genotype effect in parvalbumin fast-spiking cells, although the same report notes that somatostatin-expressing cells fire more action potentials in both models and so supply another source of the excess {cite:p}`Cepeda2013`. The two designs differ in what they manipulate (removal of ongoing input versus driven activation), in model and in stage, and the disagreement is unresolved. A parallel disagreement concerns intrinsic physiology: low-threshold-spiking interneuron membrane and synaptic properties are reported unchanged in Q175 at every disease stage, with increased burst firing in a subpopulation of those interneurons in symptomatic mice {cite:p}`Holley2018`, whereas somatostatin interneurons in BACHD mice fire at elevated rates that are normalised by reducing mutant huntingtin in those cells alone {cite:p}`Fowler2026`.
:::

That second disagreement matters for interpretation, because the BACHD result is the closest thing in this literature to a cell-autonomous test: reducing mutant huntingtin selectively in striatal somatostatin interneurons normalised their firing, implying that the protein acts within cells that do not die {cite:p}`Fowler2026`. The widely repeated claim that interneuron sparing reflects intrinsic, cell-autonomous resistance had no traceable cell-type-restricted genetic experiment behind it in the retrieved literature until tests of this kind, and both this one and its cholinergic counterpart measure physiology rather than survival {cite:p}`Fowler2026, Pancani2023`. Related cell-restricted manipulations point the same way for other classes — restricting mutant huntingtin to parvalbumin-positive neurons produces hyperactivity with impaired cortical GABA release and blunted gamma-frequency responses without loss of parvalbumin interneurons, a single-study result for which no replication was retrieved in the twelve years since {cite:p}`Dougherty2014`, and presymptomatic bioluminescent-optogenetic manipulation of parvalbumin interneurons, luciferin-driven activation of tethered light-sensing channels, improves motor coordination in R6/2 mice {cite:p}`Ikefuama2025`. Cortical fast-spiking interneuron changes are region-specific within the same model {cite:p}`Wang2023a`. For the somatostatin/NOS class specifically, direct activity measurement in vivo is almost absent: an activity-marker survey of R6/1 striatum detected c-Fos in parvalbumin interneurons but in fewer than 1% of calretinin-, cholinergic- and nNOS-expressing interneurons, too few for any further analysis {cite:p}`Cabanas2017`. No physiological recording of identified somatostatin interneurons from human HD tissue was retrieved by these searches, so every statement about their activity in HD rests on mouse models that disagree with one another.

The cholinergic interneuron, the other classically spared striatal class, supplies the cleanest demonstration that survival and function are separable. Cholinergic cells in Q140 knock-in mice have simplified dendritic arbors, with significantly fewer and shorter dendrites {cite:p}`Deng2016`; the same class shows enlarged persistent sodium currents {cite:p}`Tanimura2016`, and in zQ175 mice larger evoked inhibitory currents from indirect-pathway collaterals {cite:p}`Lim2021`; in R6/2 mice the same class shows relative resistance to loss, with reductions detectable only at later stages {cite:p}`CrevierSorbo2020`. Lowering mutant huntingtin in those cells alone, but not in cortex or indirect-pathway projection neurons, normalises corticostriatal transmission {cite:p}`Pancani2023`. A surviving population can therefore be the locus of a circuit defect; the consequences for basal ganglia output, including pallidal hyperactivity and excessive inhibition of pallidal targets in early HD {cite:p}`Callahan2022`, are developed in {ref}`sec-circuit-consequences`.

(sec-06-aggregates-expansion)=

## Aggregates, somatic expansion and the failure of burden to predict death

Molecular burden does not predict which cells die, and the clearest evidence is quantitative. In early-grade human HD, multiple perikaryal huntingtin aggregates are present in almost all cortical NADPH-diaphorase neurons and in approximately 50% of the spared striatal NADPH-diaphorase neurons, while the vulnerable spiny neurons carry comparatively few {cite:p}`Kuemmerle1999`; a subset of cortical GABAergic interneurons accumulates aggregates in a striatal-targeted model whose Cre driver is also active in cortical interneuron precursors, an incidental observation rather than a dedicated interneuron-targeted experiment {cite:p}`Gu2007`. Somatic CAG expansion fares no better as a predictor, and on this point the evidence is openly divided. Drd2-expressing striatopallidal indirect-pathway projection neurons are reported to be the more vulnerable of the two projection-neuron subtypes in HD patients {cite:p}`Lee2020`, yet cell-type-resolved measurement in human HD striatum finds no significant difference in somatic repeat expansion between direct- and indirect-pathway neurons, a single-cohort result for which no independent replication was retrieved {cite:p}`Matlik2024`. Whether cell-type-specific somatic expansion explains differential projection-neuron vulnerability is therefore unresolved: one account records the vulnerability gradient {cite:p}`Lee2020` and the other removes its most obvious molecular explanation {cite:p}`Matlik2024`. Spatial transcriptomics of human HD cortex reports that chandelier and somatostatin-positive interneurons are lost despite only modest repeat expansion or aggregation, while within the same preprint the deep-layer excitatory neurons carrying very large somatic expansions are the population reported to undergo severe loss {cite:p}`VazquezSanchez2025`. {numref}`fig-sec6-aggregate-burden-versus-cell-death` lays burden and death on a common cell-class axis; the population with abundant aggregates is the one the literature calls spared.

What tracks with vulnerability, in the models that have been tested, is projection-neuron identity itself, though the readouts differ widely, from frank degeneration {cite:p}`Noel2018, Oh2022` to spine, calcium and excitability phenotypes {cite:p}`Schmidt2020, Fjodorova2023, Goodliffe2018`. Direct-pathway D1 neurons are significantly altered in aged Q175 heterozygotes while D2 neurons are comparatively less affected, a gradient opposite to the indirect-pathway vulnerability reported in patients {cite:p}`Goodliffe2018, Lee2020`; active caspase-6 degenerates cortical and hippocampal neurons but not striatal medium spiny neurons {cite:p}`Noel2018`; extrasynaptic GluN2B signalling through DAPK1 destabilises medium spiny neuron spines {cite:p}`Schmidt2020`; reduced BCL11B disturbs calcium homeostasis in human stem-cell-derived medium spiny neurons and cortical neurons alike, with the mitochondrial and PKA-dependent calcium-transient deficits confined to the medium spiny neurons {cite:p}`Fjodorova2023`; reprogrammed patient medium spiny neurons from symptomatic donors die selectively {cite:p}`Oh2022`; and HD striatal organoids deplete lateral-ganglionic-eminence progenitors {cite:p}`Wu2024`. Mutant huntingtin reduces striatal synaptic output cell-autonomously {cite:p}`Paraskevopoulou2021`, while deleting wild-type huntingtin in adult cortex and striatum lowers projection-neuron excitability on its own {cite:p}`Barron2026`.

Measurement constraints shape all of this. Striatal interneurons are a small fraction of striatal neurons {cite:p}`Andre2018` resolved into seven transcriptomic populations {cite:p}`Wegman2023`, so surveys of HD striatum have thin interneuron coverage; the interneuron-resolved single-nuclei data retrieved here come from an ovine model {cite:p}`Jiang2024` and the accompanying single-nucleus survey from mouse striatum {cite:p}`Malaiya2021`, not from human tissue, and no human HD single-nucleus dataset with sufficient interneuron depth to test cell-autonomous transcriptional pathology in this class was retrieved, a gap taken up in {ref}`sec-human-evidence`. Human in vitro models are not yet a substitute: stem-cell striatal differentiation yields 4.5% to 12.4% calretinin interneurons alongside substantial non-striatal fractions {cite:p}`LeCann2021`, and in vivo astrocyte-to-neuron conversion in mouse, a different route to the same cells, produces mostly projection-neuron-like cells, with 8.4% parvalbumin-positive and under 5% somatostatin-, NPY- or calretinin-positive {cite:p}`Wu2020`. Viral tools do reach the class {cite:p}`AlMassadi2025`, which makes the cell-restricted experiments above feasible and their continued scarcity notable.

(sec-06-cortex-striatum)=

## Cortex versus striatum, mouse versus human

The sharpest unresolved split is regional and interspecific. Mouse models lose cortical somatostatin-immunoreactive density: progressively in R6/2 motor and anterior cingulate cortex, with somatic shrinkage of 16%, 15% and 13% in somatostatin-, parvalbumin- and VIP-expressing cells but unchanged genetically labelled numbers {cite:p}`Voelkl2022`, and in BACHD cortex, where somatostatin-positive and parvalbumin-positive interneuron counts are both reduced at postnatal day 13 but only the somatostatin deficit is still present at postnatal day 30, traced to aberrant medial ganglionic eminence neurogenesis rather than to degeneration {cite:p}`Molero2026` — consistent with the finding that conditional loss of wild-type huntingtin in subpallial lineages produces early somatostatin-positive interneuron deficits {cite:p}`Mehler2019`. Functional correlates follow, with NADPH-diaphorase staining reduced in aged male Q175 motor cortex {cite:p}`PadovanNeto2019` and, in a treatment study whose reported outcomes are behavioural rather than cellular, improved motor behaviour with reduced mortality under chronic low-dose epidermal growth factor in R6/2 mice {cite:p}`Marottoli2019`. The human cortical evidence runs the other way or not at all: the older human literature reports increased somatostatin-like immunoreactivity read as cortical sparing {cite:p}`Beal1988`, the human interneuron counts that do exist in HD cortex cover calbindin, calretinin and parvalbumin and report their loss in the anterior cingulate of mood-dominant cases {cite:p}`Kim2014`, and the only retrieved human report of cortical somatostatin interneuron loss is an unrefereed spatial-transcriptomic preprint whose interneuron claim is qualitative {cite:p}`VazquezSanchez2025`. No human stereological or molecular count of somatostatin interneurons in HD cortex was retrieved by these searches, and a caution applies even to the mouse account: the interneuron gene programme most often invoked for cortical HD pathology is predominantly expressed outside interneurons, with non-interneuron forebrain populations accounting for about 80% of neocortical PGC-1α mRNA {cite:p}`McMeekin2020`. The cortical somatostatin deficit in HD should therefore be stated as a mouse-model finding with a provisional human counterpart, not as an established human phenotype.

:::{admonition} Evidence Conflict
:class: warning
**Which cortical interneuron class is most affected, and in which species.** Human HD anterior cingulate cortex loses 80% of parvalbumin interneurons in mood-dominant cases with calbindin and calretinin losses alongside {cite:p}`Kim2014`, while R6/2 cortex shows unchanged parvalbumin density with reduced somatostatin and VIP immunoreactivity {cite:p}`Voelkl2022`; the most vulnerable cortical subtype differs between human tissue and the model. Overlaying this, human peptide measurements read cortical somatostatin as increased and spared {cite:p}`Beal1988` while a cell-resolved preprint reads somatostatin-positive interneurons as lost {cite:p}`VazquezSanchez2025`, and the canonical striatal synthesis holds the class to be relatively spared {cite:p}`Morigaki2017`. Measurement modality, region and species all separate these claims, and none of the three pairings has been resolved by a study that measures both quantities in the same material.
:::

HD therefore makes concrete what the preceding disease sections could only suggest: a population can be numerically preserved and functionally abnormal at the same time, a possibility that one model supports directly {cite:p}`Fowler2026` and another does not {cite:p}`Holley2018`, and the measurement that establishes preservation — peptide concentration, marker-positive profile count, histochemical staining — is rarely the measurement that would detect the abnormality. The striatal class discussed here is not the cortical Martinotti population defined in {ref}`sec-sst-identity` that dominates the rest of this review, which is part of why its literature has developed in near-isolation. The next disease group presents the opposite evidential problem: a condition in which the cell class is barely measured in human tissue at all, and in which the mechanistic account rests on a single mouse study — the subject of {ref}`sec-als-ftd`.
