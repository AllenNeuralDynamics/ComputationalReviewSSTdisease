(sec-conclusion)=

# Conclusion

The somatostatin system is disrupted in several neurodegenerative diseases, and in Alzheimer's disease (AD) that disruption carries a causal proposal which remains credible and unresolved. Shared selective death of the somatostatin-expressing (SST) interneuron class is a separate claim, and it is not established. Both claims are load-bearing, and the evidence assembled here supports them at different strengths.

What is firm is the direction of the signal. Somatostatin falls in affected cortex, and independent groups have reproduced that fall across four decades on four platforms:

- tissue radioimmunoassay
- cerebrospinal fluid
- cell counting
- single-nucleus sequencing

The morphological counterpart was described in the earliest human material: surviving somatostatin neurons distorted with short dendrites and truncated axons, worst where tangle and plaque indices are highest {cite:p}`ChanPalay1987`.

The single strongest modern claim is narrower and better specified. Somatostatin-annotated inhibitory neurons fall in proportional abundance in AD cortex, by a cell identification that does not depend on peptide detection ({ref}`sec-human-evidence`). Deconvolution of bulk profiles points the same way without being the same measurement: across 1037 postmortem samples SST inhibitory neurons are the cell type most strongly associated with the disease (β = −0.48, p_bonf = 8.98 × 10⁻⁹). In the same cohort family their proportions are negatively associated with each tau- and amyloid-related measure, and positively associated with the rate of cognitive decline {cite:p}`Consens2022b`.

{ref}`sec-human-evidence` licenses that claim and no more. It is also the only claim in this review for which cell identification and marker measurement come apart in human tissue.

(sec-12-what-does-not-hold)=

## What the evidence does not reach

Three inferences routinely drawn from these observations do not hold at the resolution at which they are usually stated.

The first is cell death. Every test that distinguishes a lost marker from a lost neuron comes from rodent work. Crossing a Huntington model to a reporter line reduced somatostatin immunoreactivity and left the labelled cells in place {cite:p}`Voelkl2022`. Genetic fate mapping in an epilepsy model did find the labelled population itself reduced, by a smaller amount than immunoreactive counts in comparable models report {cite:p}`Drake2024`.

In human tissue the vulnerability evidence is immunoreactive counting {cite:p}`SaizSanchez2014, SanchezMejias2019`. It stands in unresolved tension with direct peptidomic measurement in pathology-matched donors, where SST-14 is maintained in resilient brains despite amyloid burden {cite:p}`Morgan2024a`. Indeed the same cohort type can be read both ways. One single-nucleus study describes SST-positive inhibitory subtypes as carrying resistance-associated profiles, while reporting *SST* itself among the most strongly downregulated genes in AD entorhinal inhibitory neurons — log2FC = −0.52 against resilient donors and −0.41 against controls {cite:p}`Castanho2025b`.

Method-level disputes of the same shape run through the older record. Tissue radioimmunoassay found somatostatin-like immunoreactivity reduced in nine of ten cortical regions {cite:p}`Gabriel1993`, while atrophy-corrected morphometry in the same disease finds no significant difference ({ref}`sec-human-evidence`). {ref}`sec-conflicts-limitations` reports these disagreements as open, and this review does not settle them.

The second is the mechanism. The proposal that somatostatin controls amyloid-β through neprilysin is coherent {cite:p}`Saito2005`, and protein engineering aimed at the same enzyme has in vivo support: a brain-penetrant somatostatin fusion protein raises hippocampal neprilysin, with the membrane-fraction increase correlating with the fall in Aβ42 {cite:p}`Rofo2021`. But the one amyloid knock-in cross of the ligand knockout is negative on the enzyme ({ref}`sec-alzheimers`), and the account has no cell-type-resolved neprilysin measurement in human AD cortex behind it.

Cell-intrinsic accounts of why this class should be fragile sit in a similar position:

- the sleep-disruption route has been demonstrated outside amyloid pathology {cite:p}`Raven2025`
- the endoplasmic-reticulum-stress route rests on a single small-sample study, also outside amyloid pathology ({ref}`sec-alzheimers`)
- model construct confounds the comparison, since 30–76% of somatostatin-expressing neurons in hAPP transgenic hippocampus express the transgene themselves {cite:p}`Hofling2019`, while an endogenous-dosage knock-in shows no significant change in SOM density {cite:p}`Sos2020`

The third is cross-disease generality. Outside AD the human evidence is thin and mostly not cell-resolved:

- in transgenic synucleinopathy hippocampus, interneuron marker expression is largely unchanged across ages, differing only for calbindin and somatostatin at 43 weeks {cite:p}`FloresCuadrado2016`
- in Huntington's disease models the cortical picture is a marker reduction without loss of genetically labelled cells {cite:p}`Voelkl2022`, while the human striatal and cortical peptide record runs the other way and is unresolved ({ref}`sec-huntingtons`)
- in the ALS–frontotemporal spectrum the cell class is barely quantified in human tissue, and the one subtype-resolved prion-disease measurement is a preprint in knock-in mice awaiting replication {cite:p}`Bauer2022a`

"Shared" in this review means a shared reduction of the somatostatin signal, and not a demonstrated shared loss of neurons.

(sec-12-why-it-matters)=

## Why the distinction decides the target

The difference between a lost population and a degraded signalling system selects the intervention. If the cells are gone, the target is replacement. If the peptide and its dendritic inhibition are degraded in surviving cells, the target is restoration of a signal.

The circuit account in {ref}`sec-circuit-consequences` makes the second possibility consequential. A fall in somatostatin signalling degrades dendritic gating and oscillatory coordination whether or not a neuron dies. In an oligomer model both theta and gamma power fall alongside increased initial release probability at SST and parvalbumin (PV) outputs {cite:p}`Chung2020`. In addition, dystrophic tau-bearing SST neurons make up more than 40% of the hippocampal SST population of a tau–amyloid rat at 15 months, with PV plasticity compensating {cite:p}`Morrone2022`.

Replacement is correspondingly hard to aim. Transplanted medial ganglionic eminence progenitors mature into PV and SST cells in near-equal proportions {cite:p}`Yokomizo2025a`. A graft is therefore not an SST intervention.

The therapeutic record matches the weaker reading of the evidence, and not the stronger one. No intervention acting selectively on SST interneurons has been shown to benefit a neurodegenerative disease in humans.

The somatostatin receptor 4 (SSTR4) programme maps a coherent rodent pathway. A selective agonist raises cortical neprilysin expression 9.3-fold and insulin-degrading enzyme 14.8-fold at 24 h {cite:p}`Sandoval2019`, and it improves learning while lowering soluble Aβ42 oligomers through a phosphoramidon-sensitive mechanism in APPswe Tg2576 amyloid-model mice {cite:p}`Sandoval2013`. Both results come from one closely related research programme, with no independent replication outside it and no support beyond amyloid models.

Gamma entrainment, the most publicised interneuron-directed strategy, was built on driving fast-spiking PV cells instead of SST cells {cite:p}`Iaccarino2016`, then extended by sensory entrainment {cite:p}`Martorell2019`. In patients the trials tabulated in a synthesis of clinical gamma stimulation report no significant change in overall cognition tests under protocols of up to four weeks. That same synthesis also tabulates longer protocols with reported improvement, and reaches a conclusion favourable to the technique {cite:p}`DePaolis2024`.

Excitability-lowering pharmacology engages its target without delivering cognition. Low-dose levetiracetam reduced hippocampal hyperactivity and improved a hyperactivity-sensitive task, while leaving standard delayed recall unchanged in the same 17 patients {cite:p}`Bakker2012`. The model literature is consistent: levetiracetam suppresses aberrant network activity and reverses hippocampal remodelling, synaptic and cognitive deficits {cite:p}`Sanchez2012`, and restoring Nav1.1 raises gamma activity in the same model family while reducing epileptiform discharges {cite:p}`Verret2012`. But a randomized crossover trial in AD patients without seizures was halted after recruiting 8 of a planned 30, establishing tolerability and not efficacy {cite:p}`Sen2024`.

{numref}`fig-sec12-intervention-effect-versus-design-rigor` shows the pattern. Every preclinical entry reports benefit, while among the human entries the direction of reported benefit tracks the design that produced it.

(sec-12-agenda)=

## What would settle it

{ref}`sec-conflicts-limitations` ranks the missing measurements. Four of them would move the answer most:

- a marker-independent identification of somatostatin interneurons applied to human AD cortex — transcript in situ hybridisation read against a pan-GABAergic or nuclear-identity label in the same tissue. This measurement would convert the proportional depletion seen in sequencing cohorts into a statement about neurons instead of annotations
- an SST-selective causal manipulation inside a disease model rather than a healthy animal, which {ref}`sec-therapeutics` identifies as the experiment for which the field has substituted broader-population and PV-directed manipulations
- a prospective cerebrospinal fluid somatostatin study reporting discrimination statistics in place of group means. Forty years of measurement have produced group differences without a single sensitivity, specificity or area under the curve, which is why {ref}`sec-human-evidence` treats this as the weakest claim in the review
- cell-type-resolved neprilysin measurement in human AD cortex, which would test the amyloid-degradation account directly instead of by extrapolation from rodents

Two further requirements are methodological. Vulnerability and resilience cohorts need reconciling in the same donors with the same annotation, since they presently disagree about whether the somatostatin system is depleted in human AD cortex at all {cite:p}`SaizSanchez2014, Morgan2024a`. And the class-level claim of {ref}`sec-selective-vulnerability` requires one experiment — counting cells by an identity independent of somatostatin, in the same tissue in which the somatostatin signal falls. That experiment has not been done in human tissue.

The limits of this synthesis bear on how firmly any of the above can be asserted. Three of them matter here:

- one bibliographic database dominated the search, with no citation chasing, so every absence noted above is an absence in that search and not a demonstrated gap in the record
- full-text coverage was uneven: 77 of 77 for the circuit literature, against 22 of the 87 source claims behind the human postmortem and fluid evidence verified against the full text of the paper they come from. The shortfall is concentrated in closed-access work published before 2000
- searches for disconfirming results were capped, so the disagreements inventoried above are a sample of the disagreement in this field and not a census

The question this review opened with has a conditional answer. Somatostatin interneuron dysfunction is a shared feature of Alzheimer's disease, and its causal contribution rests entirely on rodent knockout and agonist work. In several other conditions it is a plausible contributor to circuit-level dysfunction.

Shared selective death of the cell class is not established. The experiments that would establish or refute it are specifiable and few in number, and none of them has been done. Until they are, the honest reading of the most reproducible neurochemical observation in neurodegeneration is that a signal has fallen — and that the field has been reading a measurement as a cell.
