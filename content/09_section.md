(sec-human-evidence)=

# Human Evidence: Postmortem Pathology, Single-Nucleus Atlases, and CSF Somatostatin as a Biomarker

The predictions carried forward from {ref}`sec-circuit-consequences` are claims about human brains, and two bodies of human measurement exist to test them: tissue taken at autopsy, and peptide measured in cerebrospinal fluid. They do not fare equally. Compositional analysis of single nuclei finds the somatostatin-annotated inhibitory population depleted in Alzheimer cortex by a method that identifies cells transcriptomically rather than by detecting the peptide {cite:p}`Mathys2023, Gabitto2024`; the depletion is placed early along the pathological trajectory by the two cohorts that resolve timing {cite:p}`Gabitto2024, Travaglini2026`; and converging, though not equivalent, support comes from deconvolution of bulk profiles against a single-nucleus reference, where the accompanying statistic is for somatostatin protein against amyloid burden {cite:p}`Cain2023`. Cerebrospinal fluid somatostatin, measured for forty years and repeatedly described as an Alzheimer marker, is reduced in Parkinson's disease, Binswanger's disease and late-life depression alike {cite:p}`Dupont1982, Sunderland1987, Strittmatter1996b, Masson1990`, is measured across diagnoses and correlated with dementia severity rather than against controls in frontotemporal dementia and Alzheimer's disease {cite:p}`Minthon1997`, is raised by medication and by shunting {cite:p}`Strittmatter1997, Poca2001`, and has never been reported with a sensitivity, specificity, area under the curve or cut-off in any study retrieved here; the series that tested discrimination directly found no diagnostic specificity, and reported no reduction in Parkinson's or Huntington's disease {cite:p}`Beal1986e`. One asymmetry must be stated before any of it is read: this is the least directly verified literature in the review. Of the 87 source claims behind this part of the review, 22 were verified against the full text of the paper they come from: the pre-2000 radioimmunoassay series on which the historical record rests is closed-access and is held here at abstract level. Retrieval was dominated by one database with no citation chasing, so every absence recorded below is an absence from these searches rather than from the literature, and the nulls named here are a top-relevance sample rather than a census.

(sec-09-postmortem-record)=

## The postmortem record: a wide range of reported deficits and two outright nulls

The quantity called "the cortical somatostatin deficit" is not one quantity. Across the retrieved postmortem series it is, variously, the density of neurons stained by an SST-28-selective antiserum {cite:p}`Gaspar1989`, the density of cells containing somatostatin messenger RNA {cite:p}`Dournaud1994`, counts of somatostatin-immunoreactive somata whose antibody isoform selectivity is not stated {cite:p}`Kumar2005, GonzalezRodriguez2021`, bulk-tissue peptide concentration measured by radioimmunoassay {cite:p}`Rossor1980, Reinikainen1987, Gabriel1993`, and receptor binding {cite:p}`Beal1985b`. Plotted together in {numref}`fig-sec9-postmortem-somatostatin-deficit-heterogeneity`, the reported reductions run from 28% to 80% depending on what was measured, and the spread is not noise around a consensus value: it is what happens when incommensurable measurands are given a common name. Reductions of 60% and 80% in cortical areas 25 and 9 came from counting SST-28-immunoreactive somata in six cases of senile dementia against five controls, in material where neuropeptide-Y-immunoreactive density was unchanged and the fall in single-labelled somatostatin neurons tracked senile plaque counts at r = -0.89, P < 0.001 {cite:p}`Gaspar1989`. A halving of somatostatin-expressing cell number in hippocampus came with p = 0.0066, t = 3.640, df = 8 and no per-group size, in a series where the area occupied by somatostatin-immunoreactive fibres was not significantly reduced and olfactory bulb was preserved {cite:p}`GonzalezRodriguez2021`. The largest figure, above 70%, is reported jointly for somatostatin- and neuropeptide-Y-positive neurons and cannot be made somatostatin-specific {cite:p}`Kumar2005`. In situ hybridisation gave -41% in frontal grey matter, -66% in frontal white matter, -44% in hippocampus and -40% in parahippocampal white matter, of which only the overall parahippocampal reduction of -59% reached significance, and the same authors concluded that the ability of cortical cells to express somatostatin messenger RNA is partially preserved {cite:p}`Dournaud1994`. Against all of this stand two studies reporting no difference in somatostatin-immunoreactive cell number at all, neither of which reports a number that could be plotted {cite:p}`Nakamura1986, Li1996b`, and one that reports significantly fewer somatostatin-immunoreactive perikarya in temporal layers II and III after correction for atrophy but no percentage {cite:p}`Davies1990`. Excluding the numberless results from the visible distribution is not a neutral act; it conditions the plotted range on having found a quantified effect.

:::{admonition} Evidence Conflict
:class: warning
Whether cortical somatostatin-immunoreactive cell density falls at all in Alzheimer-type dementia is unresolved at the level of what was counted. {cite:t}`Gaspar1989` report 60-80% lower SST-28-immunoreactive neuron density in cortical areas 9 and 25; {cite:t}`Nakamura1986` report no decrease in neocortical somatostatin-immunoreactive cell density against aged normal subjects, locating the pathology instead in swollen, plaque-associated fibres. The studies differ in antiserum specificity, in cortical area sampled, and in whether density was formally quantified, and the disagreement is only partially resolved by those differences. A second, method-level version of the same dispute is sharper still: tissue radioimmunoassay finds somatostatin-like immunoreactivity lower in nine of ten cortical regions with neuropeptide Y entirely spared {cite:p}`Gabriel1993`, while atrophy-corrected immunocytochemical morphometry in the same disease finds no significant difference in somatostatin-immunoreactive fibre length per cortical strip by a randomised method {cite:p}`Davies1990`. Peptide content per neuron, and the detectability of lightly stained cells, are the confounds neither design can exclude. Beneath both disputes sits an older and still unresolved one about whether the bulk peptide deficit exists at all: the founding radioimmunoassay report described somatostatin-like immunoreactivity in the cerebral cortex as reduced in tissue from Alzheimer-type dementia {cite:p}`Davies1980`, whereas a later series that matched cases and controls for age and postmortem delay and measured both somatostatin-like immunoreactivity and somatostatin binding in cortical areas 9 and 21 found no significant change in any disease group and concluded that somatostatinergic involvement is not a consistent primary feature {cite:p}`Whitford1988`. That is a disagreement about the same analyte measured by the same class of assay, and matching on the tissue-quality variables is what separates the two designs.
:::

The evidence that fibres rather than cell bodies carry much of the pathology is independent of this dispute and is older than it: somatostatin-positive profiles are physically incorporated into 20-50% of neuritic plaques in selected cortical regions {cite:p}`Armstrong1985`, and loss of somatostatin-immunoreactive neurons and axons in hippocampus and temporal cortex is reported as most severe in exactly the subfields carrying the highest tangle and plaque indices {cite:p}`ChanPalay1987`. Two prosomatostatin-derived peptides fall in parallel across Alzheimer cortex, which the authors read as degeneration of somatostatin neurons and terminals rather than a shift in post-translational processing {cite:p}`Beal1986c`. What no retrieved study provides is the measurement that would settle it: design-based stereological counts combined with single-nucleus profiling on the same cases. The retrieved counting studies are immunocytochemical cell-density or fibre-length measurements, several of them without design-based stereology, and they do not agree with one another {cite:p}`Davies1990, Li1996b`. Whether a falling transcript signal reflects neuron death or reduced expression per surviving neuron therefore cannot be decided from the existing counting literature.

(sec-09-disease-specificity)=

## Is the deficit specific to Alzheimer-type pathology?

Read across the neurodegenerative diagnoses sampled postmortem, the bulk-tissue deficit tracks cortical Alzheimer-type pathology rather than any narrower diagnosis - though, as the conflict below records, comparable cortical reductions are reported outside neurodegeneration altogether. Reductions of 47% in temporal cortex {cite:p}`Rossor1980`, 42% frontal, 28% temporal and 42% parietal with thalamus and putamen unchanged {cite:p}`Reinikainen1987`, and widespread cortical reductions most profound in temporal lobe with no significant subcortical change {cite:p}`Beal1986a` are joined by a reduction of approximately 40% confined to parkinsonian patients who carried Alzheimer-type cortical pathology, non-demented parkinsonian donors having normal concentrations in cerebral cortex, hippocampus, amygdala, putamen, caudate and globus pallidus {cite:p}`Beal1986d`. In progressive supranuclear palsy, somatostatin concentrations were not different from controls in any region tested, in tissue with a demonstrable cholinergic deficit {cite:p}`Epelbaum1987`, and cortical binding of a radiolabelled somatostatin analogue was unchanged in demented and non-demented parkinsonian patients and in progressive supranuclear palsy {cite:p}`Epelbaum1988`. That null sits directly against the report that somatostatin receptor concentrations are reduced to approximately 50 percent of control values in Alzheimer frontal and temporal cortex, with a 40 percent reduction in the hippocampus {cite:p}`Beal1985b`: whether somatostatin receptor binding is lost in dementing illness is unsettled, and because the two studies used different ligands in different diseases, the disagreement may be disease-specific rather than methodological. Olivopontocerebellar atrophy breaks the pattern in an instructive way: 42-58% reductions in parietal and occipital cortices and frontal cortical eye fields, normal concentrations in other cortical areas including the two temporal subdivisions that are most depleted in Alzheimer's disease, and a 46% *elevation* in the caudate nucleus, the region with that brain's most severe cholinergic deficit {cite:p}`Kish1993`.

:::{admonition} Evidence Conflict
:class: warning
Pathology-dependence and disease-specificity are not the same claim, and the retrieved evidence separates them. {cite:t}`Beal1986d` restrict the cortical reduction to parkinsonian cases carrying Alzheimer-type cortical pathology, which argues that the deficit tracks the pathology. {cite:t}`Gabriel1996` find comparable cortical somatostatin reductions in schizophrenia, a disorder without Alzheimer-type pathology, with neuropeptide Y reduced only in schizophrenia and corticotropin-releasing hormone primarily in Alzheimer's disease. Somatostatin concentration also falls in dentate gyrus and CA4 of cryptogenic temporal lobe epilepsy tissue, with increased somatostatin binding site density in the dentate gyrus against tumour-resection controls {cite:p}`Robbins1991`. The deficit therefore tracks neither neurodegeneration in general nor Alzheimer pathology exclusively, and the dispute remains open.
:::

Outside Alzheimer's disease the cellular question is untested, because the non-Alzheimer human evidence retrieved here is almost entirely fluid or bulk. Huntington's disease illustrates the cost: basal ganglia somatostatin-like immunoreactivity is significantly increased {cite:p}`Dawbarn1985`, somatostatin-28(1-12) immunoreactivity is increased in caudate and putamen with cortical areas unchanged {cite:p}`Beal1985a`, somatostatin binding site density is markedly reduced in the same structures {cite:p}`Palacios1990`, and a cross-disease review describes the co-localised somatostatin/neuropeptide-Y population as selectively preserved {cite:p}`Beal1986b` - a configuration discussed in {ref}`sec-huntingtons`, and one in which peptide and receptor move in opposite directions because they are different quantities. For Parkinson's disease the comparable cortical evidence is proteomic deconvolution, which finds reduced somatostatin interneuron signatures in layers 5 and 6 (p = 0.0006) alongside corticofugal projection neurons {cite:p}`Dick2024`, and a circumscribed downregulation of somatostatin expression in the anterior olfactory nucleus anterior division (P = 0.0113) {cite:p}`UbedaBanon2017`. Quantitative somatostatin cell counts in amyotrophic lateral sclerosis, frontotemporal lobar degeneration, Huntington's disease or dementia with Lewy bodies cortex were not retrieved by these searches, and neither was an atlas-scale subtype analysis for progressive supranuclear palsy, multiple system atrophy or amyotrophic lateral sclerosis.

(sec-09-single-nucleus)=

## Single-nucleus atlases: the strongest claim in the review, and its fragilities

The modern cohorts are the strongest human evidence here, and they are strong for a specific reason: they identify cells by transcriptomic identity rather than by peptide immunoreactivity, so they are not vulnerable to the peptide-content confound that the counting literature cannot exclude. A 2.3-million-cell prefrontal atlas of 427 individuals found, by quasi-binomial regression, decreased relative abundance of the entire somatostatin inhibitory subclass and of all three of its vulnerable subtypes in individuals with higher levels of Alzheimer pathology, with the same subclass reduced in individuals scoring low on global cognition {cite:p}`Mathys2023`. In 84 donors profiled by single-nucleus RNA sequencing, single-nucleus ATAC sequencing, spatial transcriptomics and quantitative neuropathology, superficial Sst interneurons were among the types lost during the early epoch of a continuous pseudo-progression score, with the decline consistent across transcriptomic and spatial modalities {cite:p}`Gabitto2024`. A deconvolution of 638 bulk-profiled individuals against a 24-donor single-nucleus reference recovered a somatostatin inhibitory subset among the populations associated with Alzheimer's disease, and somatostatin protein associated with amyloid burden at P < 0.01 {cite:p}`Cain2023`. Mega-analysis across bulk and single-nucleus cohorts puts the somatostatin interneuron as the most strongly Alzheimer-associated cell type, at beta = -0.48 and Bonferroni-corrected p = 8.98 x 10^-9, while noting that the difference was not significant in all regions {cite:p}`Consens2022b`. Convergent, orthogonal signals follow: somatostatin is among the four downregulated feature genes selected by machine learning in middle temporal gyrus {cite:p}`Wang2024`, significantly lower in precuneus by sequencing and quantitative PCR {cite:p}`Guennewig2021`, recovered among Alzheimer-correlated proteins by unbiased proteomics {cite:p}`Sathe2020`, and localised by peptidomics to somatostatin interneurons including the layer 3/5 SST MAFB population independently described as vulnerable {cite:p}`Morgan2024a`.

Four caveats bound that convergence, and {numref}`fig-sec9-single-nucleus-cohort-convergence` is built to display them rather than to average them away. First, cell-type granularity differs between cohorts - subclass and subtype, supertype, high-resolution type, deconvolution-inferred subset - so "the somatostatin population" denotes a different entity in each row {cite:p}`Mathys2023, Gabitto2024, Cain2023, Mathys2024`, a resolution problem made concrete by patch-seq characterisation showing that somatostatin transcriptomic types map onto distinct morpho-electric phenotypes {cite:p}`Lee2023b`. Second, the agreement is not fully independent, and in two separate ways: the 427-donor prefrontal atlas and the 638-individual deconvolution draw on the same parent study population, so their concordance is not independent replication {cite:p}`Mathys2023, Cain2023`; and two of these reports come from the same group, the later describing its result as consistent with the earlier {cite:p}`Mathys2023, Mathys2024`. Third, the pathology axes differ - level of Alzheimer pathology, a continuous pseudo-progression score, tangle density, amyloid burden, categorical stage - so "depleted in Alzheimer's disease" is anchored to a different predictor in each study. Fourth, and most consequentially, the signal is sensitive to the statistical unit: the same single-nucleus data that yield a significant group difference in Sst neuron number, F(3,81) = 4.381, p = 0.0066, yield no significant difference in Sst proportion, t(6) = 1.333, p = 0.2309, nor in the Sst Chodl proportion, in the smaller stage-wise comparison {cite:p}`Joshi2024`. A multiregion atlas of 1.3 million cells from 48 individuals identifies inhibitory subtypes depleted with high tangle density, classified as vulnerable at a Benjamini-Hochberg-adjusted P value below 0.05, but does not name a somatostatin population among the subtypes it reports, and is therefore evidence about inhibitory vulnerability rather than about somatostatin {cite:p}`Mathys2024`.

:::{admonition} Provenance
:class: note
**Source:** "During the early epoch, superficial Sst, Sncg and Lamp5 interneurons were lost." {cite:p}`Gabitto2024`
**Replication:** convergent direction in three further human cohorts of different design {cite:p}`Mathys2023, Cain2023, Travaglini2026`
:::

Two further results sharpen what is being claimed. Depletion is reported earliest, in preclinical donors with minimal pathology, for specific subsets of Sst, Lamp5, Vip, Sncg and Pvalb interneurons together {cite:p}`Travaglini2026`, so earliness is not a somatostatin-specific property. And preservation of somatostatin-positive cells, not their loss, is what distinguishes cognitively resilient donors — the vulnerable-versus-resilient dispute of {ref}`sec-04-vulnerable-or-resilient`, adjudicated here with cohort-level data: the entorhinal reduction replicates in two somatostatin subclasses in demented donors but not in resilient ones {cite:p}`Castanho2025b`, mitochondrial bioenergetic signatures in somatostatin neurons are linked to resilience despite amyloid and tau pathology {cite:p}`Cheng2025`, and somatostatin sits inside an activity-dependent, neuropentraxin-2-correlated programme preserved in controls and lost in disease {cite:p}`Lao2025`. Against that, the cell-type signature replicates only partially across population groups, where vulnerable SST+MAFB+ neurons showed a trend rather than a significant depletion {cite:p}`Luquez2026`, and the claim that somatostatin interneurons are the *most* vulnerable interneuron class in human Alzheimer cortex is widely repeated but was not traced in these searches to a primary quantitative within-tissue comparison against parvalbumin and vasoactive intestinal peptide populations. The one retrieved study making an explicit somatostatin-versus-parvalbumin comparison in human tissue is in the Parkinson's disease amygdala and finds the opposite ordering, with parvalbumin-positive cells co-localising alpha-synuclein in 68.44% of cases against 6.98% for somatostatin-positive cells {cite:p}`FloresCuadrado2017` - a single unreplicated series, nine years old, and the only direct cross-marker ranking available.

(sec-09-csf)=

## Cerebrospinal fluid somatostatin: forty years without a discrimination statistic

Assembled as a biomarker evaluation rather than as a sequence of group differences, the cerebrospinal fluid literature fails on every axis that matters, and {numref}`fig-sec9-csf-somatostatin-is-not-specific` is laid out to show why. No retrieved study reports a sensitivity, a specificity, an area under the curve or a validated cut-off; the series that addressed discrimination directly concluded that concentrations in Alzheimer's disease overlap both normal subjects and multi-infarct dementia so heavily that the measure has no diagnostic specificity {cite:p}`Beal1986e`. Reductions are reported in Parkinson's disease at 88.0 against 147.3 pg per millilitre in controls {cite:p}`Dupont1982` and with P less than .02 in an aged cohort where values were lowest in untreated patients {cite:p}`Masson1990`; in Alzheimer-type dementia at 35% below controls in 75 patients {cite:p}`Reinikainen1987`, at 14.6 against 26.7 pg per millilitre relative to other neurological patients {cite:p}`Serby1984`, and at 17.5 pg/mL in dementia of the Alzheimer type and 16.4 pg/mL with extrapyramidal signs against 23.1 pg/mL in age-matched controls {cite:p}`Atack1988`; in severe Alzheimer-type dementia at 18.6 and in Binswanger's disease at 19.7 against 30.5 fmol/ml in controls {cite:p}`Strittmatter1996b`; with concentrations correlating negatively with dementia severity in Alzheimer's disease in a series of 34 Alzheimer and 22 frontotemporal dementia patients that included no control group and so reports no reduction against controls {cite:p}`Minthon1997`; in Huntington's disease patients and their symptom-free offspring {cite:p}`Cramer1981`; equally in Alzheimer's disease, multi-infarct dementia and normal-pressure hydrocephalus with no difference between them {cite:p}`Molins1991`; and in late-life depression as deeply as in dementia {cite:p}`Sunderland1987`. A contemporary review of this literature drew the same conclusion from the same primary series: altered fluid concentrations have been reported across Alzheimer's disease, major depression, Huntington's chorea, multiple sclerosis, schizophrenia and Parkinson's disease, while tissue deficits are the more disease-restricted finding {cite:p}`Bissette1992`.

:::{admonition} Evidence Conflict
:class: warning
Three disagreements run through this literature, and none is resolved. On specificity, {cite:t}`Sunderland1987` find somatostatin low in both Alzheimer's disease and late-life depression, while {cite:t}`Banki1992` find no significant difference across major depression, mania, anxiety and dementia groups in whom corticotropin-releasing hormone was raised in major depression and in dementia with depression relative to neurological controls without psychiatric disorder; both are small single-centre radioimmunoassay series with different control groups. On generality, {cite:t}`Reinikainen1987` report a 35% reduction across 75 patients, while {cite:t}`Urakami1989` find no significant difference in gradually and very slowly progressing cases, the deficit being confined to rapidly declining ones, and an earlier series found only a slight reduction in presenile and none in senile-onset disease {cite:p}`Francis1984`. On severity tracking, somatostatin-like immunoreactivity was reported to correlate with dementia severity {cite:p}`Soininen1984` and, in another cohort, to be reduced but unrelated to severity and stable across progression {cite:p}`Atack1988`. Even the direction of the Parkinson's disease change is contested, and the cleanest statement of it holds the assay class constant: {cite:t}`Jolkkonen1986` report somatostatin-like immunoreactivity lower than in controls (p less than 0.01), with the lowest values in demented parkinsonian patients, while {cite:t}`Espino1995` report a significant increase in untreated early parkinsonian syndrome with severe memory impairment. Both measure cerebrospinal fluid by radioimmunoassay in comparably sized early-stage cohorts, so disease stage and treatment status rather than assay class are the candidate explanations - a disagreement that remains open and bears directly on the specificity argument in {ref}`sec-parkinsons-lewy-body`.
:::

Three structural problems make these numbers non-comparable even where they agree. Units are incompatible between laboratories, picograms per millilitre against femtomoles per millilitre, and the assays measure a mixture of molecular forms, so no single molecular weight licenses a conversion {cite:p}`Atack1988, Serby1984, Strittmatter1996b`; no cross-calibration between the in-house radioimmunoassays was reported in any retrieved study. Cross-reactivity is characterised almost nowhere: one tissue study reports an assay that does not cross-react with cortistatin-like immunoreactivity {cite:p}`Grouselle1998`, only one resolves molecular forms by chromatography {cite:p}`Strittmatter1997` while a second reports somatostatin molecular forms without naming the separation method {cite:p}`Strittmatter1996b`, and a small mass-spectrometric series argues that a radioimmunoassay decrease may reflect altered synthesis or extracellular processing generating fragments the assay may or may not detect {cite:p}`Nilsson2001`. No retrieved study compares radioimmunoassay against mass spectrometry on the same samples, and pre-analytical variables - collection tube, gradient position, freeze-thaw, time to freezing - are not reported. And the measure is pharmacologically and surgically movable: long-term neuroleptics significantly raised somatostatin-like immunoreactivity in one dementia cohort {cite:p}`Strittmatter1997`, values were lowest in untreated parkinsonian patients {cite:p}`Masson1990`, cholinesterase-inhibitor responders showed a significant rise and non-responders a significant fall {cite:p}`Alhainen1991`, and shunting in normal-pressure hydrocephalus significantly increased somatostatin in all patients while neuropsychological performance did not significantly improve {cite:p}`Poca2001`. That last result - unreplicated in twenty-five years, and resting on a single series of 14 patients - is the strongest retrieved argument that the fluid measure partly indexes cerebrospinal fluid dynamics rather than interneuron integrity: a value that rises again after a shunt, with no accompanying neuropsychological improvement, is not straightforwardly a count of surviving cells.

Within-cohort correlations do not rescue the claim. Somatostatin-like immunoreactivity correlated with dementia score at r = -0.65 in one series {cite:p}`Strittmatter1997` and negatively with severity at p < 0.013 in another whose recorded cohort comprises only Alzheimer's disease and frontotemporal dementia patients, with no control group reported {cite:p}`Minthon1997`; a positive correlation with cognitive functioning held across all 47 subjects of a three-group study but not within any diagnostic group {cite:p}`Sunderland1987`; in a memory-clinic cohort somatostatin tracked amyloid-beta 1-42 (p = 0.01) and blood total cholesterol (p = 0.03) rather than tau, with hypertension and diabetes among its univariate associates {cite:p}`Duron2018`. Reduced fluid somatostatin has been tied to parietal glucose hypometabolism on positron emission tomography {cite:p}`Tamminga1987`, to cortical concentrations measured in the same patients {cite:p}`Gomez1986b` and, in a second series, to parallel tissue and fluid deficits measured in different patient sets - six donors with histopathologically confirmed disease for cortex against 75 patients for fluid {cite:p}`Reinikainen1987`, and - in a single report, thirty-eight years old and unreplicated - to electroencephalographic slowing, specifically decreased beta activity and a decreased mean frequency in the combined alpha and theta range {cite:p}`Soininen1988`, an association interpreted through dendritic inhibition in {ref}`sec-circuit-consequences`. The most consequential unreplicated claim in this literature is also its oldest proposal: that the deficit reflects impaired prohormone processing, with lowered somatostatin-14 and a significant increase in the unprocessed 15 kDa precursor, advanced forty years ago as an antemortem diagnostic test and never, in these searches, retested {cite:p}`Gomez1986a`. Beyond one cohort's resampling during progression, in which concentrations did not change significantly {cite:p}`Atack1988`, and the pre- and post-shunt measurements above {cite:p}`Poca2001`, no retrieved study reports a serial within-individual trajectory, and none tests whether a baseline value predicts conversion from mild cognitive impairment to dementia.

(sec-09-tissue-quality)=

## Tissue quality, peptide turnover and the limits of the record

Postmortem interval, brain pH, agonal state and RNA integrity are measurable confounds whose uniformity across diagnostic groups is itself disputed, and they are treated as a structural limitation of this literature in {ref}`sec-conflicts-limitations`. Only some of the retrieved series document the relevant case-control matching at all - six age- and postmortem-delay-matched brains in the transcript study {cite:p}`Dournaud1994`, and atrophy correction across six control and six Alzheimer cases in the morphometric study {cite:p}`Davies1990` - and no somatostatin-specific degradation curve exists in the retrieved literature with which to bound the reported effect sizes. What does exist is evidence that the analyte's own turnover is altered by the disease: the metabolic half-life of somatostatin-28 is significantly reduced in Alzheimer-type dementia cortex {cite:p}`Weber1992`, so a measured concentration confounds synthesis with catabolism. Two mechanistic negatives further constrain interpretation. The content and enzymatic activity of the mature prohormone convertase 2 are similar in Alzheimer patients and controls, so convertase failure does not explain the cortical deficit; the same study holds that the deficit arises without major loss of somatostatin-containing neurons, a reading that sits against the depletion interpretation the single-nucleus cohorts support {cite:p}`WinskySommerer2003`; and neprilysin expression is unaltered in the Alzheimer temporal lobe despite reduction of somatostatin, cortistatin and their receptors, placing the amyloid-degradation defect upstream of neprilysin transcription {cite:p}`Gahete2010`. A final confound is not disease at all: somatostatin transcript falls in dorsolateral prefrontal cortex with normal ageing, significantly in both sexes in layers 1-5 {cite:p}`Sloan2026`, which is the baseline any case-control difference must clear and the reason {ref}`sec-selective-vulnerability` treats ageing as the relevant comparator rather than as background.

(sec-09-licensed)=

## What the human evidence licenses

Stated precisely, the human record supports one claim: the proportional abundance of somatostatin-annotated inhibitory neurons is reduced in Alzheimer cortex, in cohorts of different size, region and design, by cell identification that does not depend on peptide detection {cite:p}`Mathys2023, Gabitto2024`; the reduction appears early along pathological trajectories in the two cohorts that resolve timing {cite:p}`Gabitto2024, Travaglini2026`; and deconvolution-based analyses of bulk profiles point the same way without being the same measurement {cite:p}`Cain2023, Consens2022b`. Three things it does not support. It does not support a diagnostic fluid marker: forty years of cerebrospinal fluid measurement have produced group differences, overlapping distributions, no discrimination statistic, cross-diagnostic reductions and a measure that rises after shunting {cite:p}`Beal1986e, Sunderland1987, Molins1991, Poca2001`, and biomarker-grade endpoints of the kind {ref}`sec-therapeutics` would require for any trial do not exist in this literature. It does not support a demonstrated mechanism of cell death: the counting studies disagree with the radioimmunoassay studies {cite:p}`Davies1990, Gabriel1993, Nakamura1986`, the convertase and neprilysin routes return negatives {cite:p}`WinskySommerer2003, Gahete2010`, and a human-versus-mouse comparison finds somatostatin, GAD1 and neurogranin reduced in human cortex but unchanged in 3xTgAD mice {cite:p}`Gleichmann2012` - a single contested comparison in one transgenic model rather than a species-level result, but one that bears on where mechanism is usually tested. And it does not support a cross-disease human pattern: outside Alzheimer's disease the retrieved evidence is almost entirely fluid, bulk or proteomic {cite:p}`Dick2024, Dupont1982, Dawbarn1985`, the exceptions being two small Parkinson's disease immunohistochemical series {cite:p}`UbedaBanon2017, FloresCuadrado2017`, and disease-specificity of somatostatin neuron depletion is untested at cellular resolution. Genetic and correlative work points in the same direction without closing it - a somatostatin variant raising Alzheimer risk with an odds ratio of 1.42 overall and 2.05 within apolipoprotein E epsilon-4 carriers, reported once nineteen years ago {cite:p}`Vepsalainen2007`; cortical somatostatin concentrations of 0.71 against 0.42 ng/mg wet weight between epsilon-2/3 and epsilon-4 carriers within a mixed clinical cohort, reported once twenty-eight years ago {cite:p}`Grouselle1998`; covariation of cortical somatostatin expression with mitochondrial and oxidative-stress transcripts {cite:p}`Liu2026`; and a polygenic score built to index somatostatin interneuron function, applied to a late-life cohort {cite:p}`DosSantos2026`. If somatostatin neurons are depleted early in human Alzheimer cortex and the peptide system is degraded around them, an intervention aimed at this system should help. {ref}`sec-therapeutics` examines whether any has, and what the pattern of failures implies for the causal claim.
