(sec-conflicts-limitations)=

# Conflicting Evidence, Model Limitations, and Open Questions

The interventions reviewed in {ref}`sec-therapeutics` failed most clearly where the causal chain had been extrapolated rather than demonstrated: from a reduced somatostatin signal, to a lost cell, to a circuit lesion a receptor agonist or an entrainment protocol might reverse. The reasons that chain was never closed are methodological, and they are visible in the primary literature. Reported change in the somatostatin signal runs from no detectable change in cell number {cite:p}`Futacsi2025` and no detectable change in density {cite:p}`Sos2020`, through 63% of hilar somatostatin-immunoreactive neurons surviving at the septal end and 21% at the temporal end after kainate {cite:p}`Jolkkonen1997`, to decreases of 57% and 61% in perirhinal cortex whose status as cell counts or as densities the retrieved text does not establish {cite:p}`SanchezMejias2019`. The spread tracks counting design only in part: within design-based stereology the reported values run from no change {cite:p}`Futacsi2025, Sos2020` to frank degeneration {cite:p}`Loreth2012`, and the immunoreactive readouts extend to those perirhinal decreases {cite:p}`SanchezMejias2019`. The one design here that identifies the cells independently of the peptide, genetic fate mapping, reports a statistically significant but small reduction {cite:p}`Drake2024`. A current synthesis still presents early excitation-inhibition imbalance as driven by loss *or* dysfunction of somatostatin and parvalbumin interneurons without resolving which {cite:p}`Okechukwu2026`, while the counting literature warns that neuron-number estimates for one structure diverge between investigators using nominally the same method {cite:p}`HerculanoHouzel2015`.

(sec-11-measurement)=

## Measurement decides the answer

The quantitative core of the problem is set out in {numref}`fig-sec11-measurement-determines-the-answer`. Design-based stereology in TgF344-AD rats at 7-8 months found no change in the number of parvalbumin-, calretinin- or somatostatin-immunoreactive neurons, with reduction restricted to cholecystokinin cells {cite:p}`Futacsi2025`; genetic fate mapping in a DGC-Pten knockout mouse found hippocampal eGFP-SST interneuron density reduced from 2,176 ± 97 to 1,901 ± 68 cells/mm3 (p = 0.0402) — a density measure, which in a model with an expanded dentate molecular layer is not interchangeable with a change in cell number, and a change of a different order from the marker-based reports {cite:p}`Drake2024`; and in the same amyloid literature a knock-in line carrying APP at endogenous dosage showed no significant change in somatostatin interneuron density in CA1 stratum oriens at 24 months, against a wild-type arm of 300 (270-350) cells/mm3 in 3 mice {cite:p}`Sos2020`. Immunoreactivity counts return much larger numbers: 57% and 61% decreases in somatostatin-positive neurons in perirhinal areas 36 and 35 of 6-month-old APP/PS1 mice, alongside 91% in Braak V-VI human cases {cite:p}`SanchezMejias2019`, and after kainate-induced seizures only 63% of hilar somatostatin-immunoreactive neurons surviving at the septal end of the rat hippocampus and 21% at the temporal end {cite:p}`Jolkkonen1997`. The one study here whose denominator is a pan-GABAergic population rather than the somatostatin population reported that kainate-treated rats lose 13% of their GAD-positive dentate cells, of which 83% of the missing cells were somatostatin-immunoreactive, against 52% loss of GAD-negative hilar neurons (putative mossy cells) {cite:p}`Buckmaster1999`. Two amyloid models analysed with one stereological protocol in a single laboratory gave opposite answers - about 30% fewer calretinin-positive and more than 40% fewer parvalbumin-positive neurons in plaque-bearing 5XFAD mice, no detectable reduction in the non-plaque-forming Tg4-42 line {cite:p}`Giesers2020` - while stereology in a triple-transgenic overexpression line found frank degeneration of GABAergic septo-hippocampal projection neurons and their interneuron targets {cite:p}`Loreth2012`. Neither sorting resolves the spread. Within design-based stereology the values run from no change {cite:p}`Futacsi2025` through reductions of about 30% in calretinin and more than 40% in parvalbumin populations {cite:p}`Giesers2020` to frank degeneration of GABAergic septo-hippocampal neurons and their interneuron targets in a triple-transgenic overexpression line {cite:p}`Loreth2012` — the direct counterpart of the endogenous-dosage knock-in null {cite:p}`Sos2020`, from which it differs in construct rather than readout; within immunoreactive soma counts they run from that same null to decreases of 57% and 61% {cite:p}`SanchezMejias2019`. Counting design and construct class each account for part of the spread, and no entry in this set controls both.

Ageing sets the scale against which any disease effect must be read, and it supplies the first clean case of two laboratories contradicting each other over the same molecule and species. Pre-prosomatostatin mRNA in 25-month-old rats fell by 49% in frontal cortex, 80% in parietal cortex and 69% in striatum with no change in beta-actin gene expression, yet showed no statistical difference in hypothalamus {cite:p}`Florio1991`; relative somatostatin gene expression, somatostatin mRNA against poly A+ RNA, in male Fischer 344 rats was unchanged in cortex across 3-4, 12-14 and 22 months (0.54 ± 0.11, 0.60 ± 0.08, 0.51 ± 0.04) but roughly 50% of young levels in hypothalamus, which in situ hybridisation attributed to decreased expression within specific nuclei rather than to loss of somatostatin-containing neurons {cite:p}`SONNTAG1990`. Each study's decline is the other's null, in the other's region. Aged rat hippocampus shows interneuron marker transcripts declining differentially and inversely tracking alpha1 GABA-A subunit upregulation {cite:p}`Vela2003`, and both somatostatin mRNA and immunopositive cell number falling while parvalbumin number is preserved {cite:p}`Gavilan2007`. Marker signal and cell presence also dissociate within one tissue: somatostatin-immunoreactive axons were exuberant in the sea lion dentate gyrus despite loss of immunopositive hilar somata {cite:p}`Buckmaster2014`, a large Dlx-expressing population survives but turns hypoactive in pilocarpine epilepsy {cite:p}`Matringhen2025`, and somatostatin and parvalbumin immunolabelling was indistinguishable from wild type in Huntington's models carrying prominent nuclear inclusions {cite:p}`Menalled2000`. A marker reduction of disease magnitude is therefore available from ageing, axonal redistribution or functional silencing alone.

:::{admonition} Evidence Conflict
:class: warning
**Magnitude of somatostatin interneuron depletion after kainate-induced seizures.** {cite:t}`Buckmaster1999` attribute 83% of missing GAD-positive dentate neurons to somatostatin-immunoreactive cells while reporting only 13% loss of the GAD-positive population; {cite:t}`Jolkkonen1997` report 63% survival of somatostatin-immunoreactive hilar neurons at the septal end and 21% at the temporal end. The disagreement is in the denominator and the septotemporal sampling position rather than in the biology. **Whether genetic reporter labelling confirms or refutes apparent somatostatin depletion** turns on the disease model rather than on the labelling strategy, and the two reports are worth stating side by side: labelling GABAergic interneurons genetically, by crossing R6/2 Huntington mice to a GAD2-Cre driver and a Cre-dependent tdTomato reporter, showed that reduced somatostatin and vasoactive intestinal peptide marker immunoreactivity was not accompanied by loss of the labelled cells {cite:p}`Voelkl2022`, whereas eGFP-SST fate mapping in a Pten/mTOR epilepsy model found the labelled population itself reduced in density {cite:p}`Drake2024`. Both identified cells independently of the peptide, so the divergence tracks the disease model rather than the labelling strategy; the Huntington result is a single report in the material retrieved here. **Whether amyloid pathology reduces somatostatin interneuron number** remains open: stereology in TgF344-AD rats found no change in somatostatin-immunoreactive cell number {cite:p}`Futacsi2025`, whereas counts in APP/PS1 mouse olfactory bulb reported decreased somatostatin-positive cells and increased parvalbumin cells, with the calretinin result stated inconsistently inside that paper, whose summary reports an increase in transgenic animals while its co-localisation section reports a decrease {cite:p}`DeLaRosaPrieto2016`; species, construct, region and counting method all differ between them, and no study resolving them within one laboratory was retrieved by these searches.

(sec-11-genetic-access)=

## Genetic access is neither complete nor specific

The fate-mapping argument that separates marker downregulation from cell death is only as good as the driver line carrying it, and the error runs in both directions, as {numref}`fig-sec11-genetic-label-fidelity` shows. In situ hybridisation against Sst mRNA in SST-IRES-Cre;tdTomato hippocampus found only 48/127 (37.8%) reporter-positive cells in the CA3 pyramidal cell layer to be Sst-expressing, against 82/100 (82%) in stratum oriens and 61/74 (82.4%) in stratum radiatum - specificity that is lamina-dependent within one subfield {cite:p}`MullerKomorowska2020`. In presubiculum, somatostatin immunolabelling identified 89% of fluorescent cells in the X98 line but 71% in Sst-Cre animals, and 6 ± 3% of labelled neurons expressed both parvalbumin and somatostatin, so the marker categories overlap inside the labelled population; the authors attribute the lesser specificity of the Sst-Cre line to off-target recombination, possibly linked to maternal cre inheritance, and the retrieved text does not say what accounts for the somatostatin-negative remainder {cite:p}`Nassar2015`. The converse failure is as large: a GFP line long used as a proxy for cortical somatostatin interneurons labels 44% of the somatostatin-positive population in layer II/III and around 35% in layers IV and Va, with 29% of labelled cells co-expressing calretinin {cite:p}`Zhou2019`, so physiology from that line describes a biased subset. The same classes of failure are documented beyond these lines. reports that Cx3cr1-Cre microglial drivers label scattered non-target neurons brain-wide differ between nominally equivalent lines and between studies, and the study that tested two such lines directly found reporter expression largely restricted to microglia, attributing the neuronal labelling other groups reported to Cre-independent reporter leakiness {cite:p}`Zhao2019`; drivers nominally targeting one population differ in the spatiotemporal pattern of recombinase activity, which their authors argue would itself produce divergent phenotypes from one floxed allele {cite:p}`Zhou2021`; interneuron driver strains including Sst-Cre are maintained on a background carrying an age-related hearing-loss allele, so sensory decline confounds the ageing phenotypes they are used to study, a confound reported in an unrefereed preprint {cite:p}`Foss2024`; a further marker, glutaminyl cyclase, is expressed across subsets of five interneuron marker classes, so marker categories are not nested cleanly {cite:p}`HartlageRubsamen2009`; and in a systematic test of commercial receptor antibodies, no proposed antibody-validity criterion other than the knockout control established specificity for brain immunohistochemistry {cite:p}`Baek2013`. The figures quantified in {ref}`sec-sst-identity` therefore bound every rodent count here that is made through a driver line; the immunoreactivity counts carry the separate error of marker downregulation. Two gaps follow: no systematic region-by-region and age-by-age audit of Sst-Cre fidelity was retrieved by these searches, and no study re-measuring reporter specificity or coverage under amyloid, tau or polyglutamine pathology was retrieved at all - so the control is circular where it is most load-bearing, since if Sst transcription falls in disease, recombination history and current marker status diverge.

(sec-11-construct-dosage)=

## Construct and dosage as a predictor of phenotype

In the material retrieved here, reported interneuron phenotypes sort more consistently by model class than by pathology, although no study compares the two directly, and the proximate candidate is dosage, as set out in {numref}`fig-sec11-model-construct-and-transgene-dosage`. A knock-in line showed similar full-length APP in knock-in and background-strain cortex at 3, 6 and 9 months, confirming physiological expression {cite:p}`Yeapuri2025`; a genomic human MAPT transgene under its native promoter produced less than a 35% increase over endogenous mouse tau {cite:p}`Guo2013`; and a widely used tauopathy line is described in the review literature as carrying P301L tau at about 13 times endogenous levels, reducible to about 3 times with doxycycline, a reduction that reversed behavioural impairment while neurofibrillary tangles continued to accumulate {cite:p}`Sahara2023`. Transgene integration in such lines also disrupts endogenous genes at the insertion site, which is why the field has been urged to sequence every overexpression line in use before its phenotypes are read as amyloid effects {cite:p}`Sasaguri2022`. Overexpression further distorts the protein's cellular distribution, since endogenous APP is enriched in GABAergic interneurons rather than uniformly expressed {cite:p}`Rice2020`, and conclusions about amyloid modifiers rest almost entirely on lines whose non-physiological promoters make APP processing unlike the human situation {cite:p}`Xie2021, SchedinWeiss2020`; where and when the transgene is active matters as much as how much of it there is, since adult-onset human APP expression produces region-specific rather than uniform hyperexcitability {cite:p}`Goettemoeller2024`.

The behavioural phenotype against which interneuron changes are usually interpreted inherits the same instability, and whether App knock-in mice develop behavioural and glial phenotypes at all is itself disputed and unresolved: one series reports negligible cognitive, locomotor and microglial change in AppNL/NL mice despite increased insoluble amyloid-beta {cite:p}`Salas2018`, while the opposing report describes cognitive and emotional alterations in App knock-in mice and argues that APP overexpression produces memory impairment without amyloid deposition in some lines {cite:p}`Sakakibara2018`. Allele number (NL versus NL-G-F), age and test battery differ between them, the sentence recorded for the second study speaks to overexpressing rather than knock-in lines, and no arbitrating study was retrieved by these searches. A Y-maze deficit reported in one laboratory was absent in another {cite:p}`Jacob2019`; one knock-in line gave opposite anxiety readings in two tasks run on the same animals {cite:p}`Pervolaraki2019`; and strain background sets the baseline against which all of this is measured, documented not as a behavioural baseline but as strain-dependent striatal and nigral interneuron protein levels in relation to MPTP susceptibility {cite:p}`Bhaduri2018`; and the absence of overt degeneration in knock-in mice is partly a species limitation, since an App knock-in rat lost 9.11% of brain weight by 12 months and 16.09% by 22 months {cite:p}`Pang2021`. Construct-specific limitations of the same kind are documented for every existing TDP-43 nuclear-localisation-signal model {cite:p}`Mitra2025`. No multi-site or pre-registered replication of App knock-in cognitive phenotypes was retrieved by these searches, and no study counting somatostatin interneurons in an overexpression line and a knock-in line side by side with one protocol, one age series and one observer was retrieved either - so the central model-validity claim of this literature, that interneuron depletion is an overexpression artifact, rests on exactly the cross-study inference that the counting-variance literature says is unreliable.

(sec-11-human-tissue)=

## Human tissue confounds and cross-species transfer

On the human side the first confound is that tissue quality is not a nuisance variable but a measured effect. A 3 h postmortem interval alone generated 79 upregulated and 53 downregulated neuronal differentially expressed genes and obscured genotype effects in a tauopathy line {cite:p}`Olney2025`; in human brain banks, 96% of samples from autopsies under five hours reached an RNA integrity number of 7.5 or more against 74% from longer intervals {cite:p}`Birdsill2010`; brain pH was lower in Alzheimer cases than controls (6.4 versus 6.5) and correlated positively with measurable mRNA for every gene examined except GFAP {cite:p}`Preece2003`; and pH and RNA integrity each exerted gene-set-specific effects in a 13-brain schizophrenia microarray series that the cited study summarises from its authors' own earlier work, the cited study itself covering 25 schizophrenia, 10 bipolar and 21 control brains, so unadjusted comparisons confound disease signal with tissue state {cite:p}`Hatano2025`. Whether those confounds act uniformly is disputed: RNA integrity varied with both diagnosis (p = 0.0350) and region (p = 0.0142) in Alzheimer's disease but not in Huntington's or Parkinson's disease {cite:p}`Highet2021`, implying diagnosis-dependent confounding that no single covariate removes, whereas the pH-expression correlations were reported to hold consistently across all groupings of pathology and sex {cite:p}`Preece2003`, which would make the confound uniformly correctable. The two have not been reconciled. The question is sharpest for somatostatin, because neuropeptides degrade rapidly after death unless enzymes are inactivated by in vivo heat stabilisation {cite:p}`Salviati2025` - a control unavailable in autopsy material - and because pH and lactate shifts also occur in unmedicated mouse models, so they are not purely agonal artefacts {cite:p}`Hagihara2017`. No dose-response curve for somatostatin peptide or Sst transcript against postmortem interval, brain pH or agonal duration in human tissue was retrieved by these searches, so the reported human deficits can be neither corrected nor bounded.

Cohort composition and the choice of statistical unit compound the problem. In an autopsy series of rapidly progressive dementia, concomitant pathologies were present in 117 of 160 cases (73%) and clinicopathological agreement outside prion disease was 21% against 94% within it {cite:p}`GrauRivera2015`; white matter interstitial neurons have been quantified in only a handful of whole-brain series {cite:p}`Zouridakis2023`, and fall outside cortical counts as those are conventionally bounded; and in a postmortem schizophrenia cohort a composite index summing GAD67 and SST mRNA alterations was lower in 80.4% of cases and was associated with final educational attainment (adjusted odds ratio 1.44, p = 0.022) {cite:p}`Dienel2025` - the analytic unit partly determines what a postmortem comparison reports. Against that background, the human marker-count evidence for preferential somatostatin vulnerability - p = 0.0001 for somatostatin and p = 0.013 for calretinin cells colocalising with amyloid-beta in piriform cortex, with parvalbumin prevalence increased {cite:p}`SaizSanchez2014`, and a model counterpart in which somatostatin cells colocalise heavily with amyloid in olfactory cortex {cite:p}`SaizSanchez2012` - stands in unresolved tension with direct peptidomic measurement in pathology-matched cases, where SST-14 is maintained in the resilient brain despite amyloid burden {cite:p}`Morgan2024a`, a cohort argued at length in {ref}`sec-alzheimers`. The two sides differ in modality, immunoreactive cell counts against peptide quantification, and in how the cohort was stratified, by diagnosis against cognition at matched pathology; neither measurement adjudicates the other. Cortical peptide change in Huntington's disease runs the other way, with somatostatin increased in a minority of areas, cholecystokinin elevated 57-153%, and no cortical peptide change in rats given striatal lesions {cite:p}`Mazurek1997`, which is why the comorbidity and region arguments of {ref}`sec-parkinsons-lewy-body` and {ref}`sec-huntingtons` cannot be collapsed into one disease effect; the marker-versus-cell disagreements recorded for prion disease, traumatic injury and Down syndrome in {ref}`sec-als-ftd` are external replications of the same problem.

Cross-species transfer fails or holds depending on the level of description, and the two findings usually cited against each other are not in fact incompatible. Transcriptomic matching across mouse, marmoset and human motor cortex placed closely related types, among them Sncg and somatostatin subtypes, at chance-level correspondence (mean AUROC 0.50 across species against 0.84 within), while distinct types such as chandelier and Sst Chodl matched well {cite:p}`Bakken2021`; human and mouse cortex differ in interneuron class proportions (50.2 ± 2.3% versus 67.8 ± 0.9% medial-ganglionic-eminence-derived) and 18% of genes in the Sst Chodl type diverge more than tenfold {cite:p}`Hodge2019`; cross-species integration requires an investigator-supplied homology mapping and identifies at least one mouse inhibitory class not detected in human data {cite:p}`Liu2022`; and marker-defined interneuron categories differ in relative abundance between species, with calretinin rather than parvalbumin cells predominating in the claustrum of human and chimpanzee while parvalbumin density slightly exceeded calretinin in the one monkey examined {cite:p}`Pirone2014`. Yet proportional changes in input excitability during membrane hyperpolarisation are conserved between human and mouse parvalbumin interneurons despite species differences in absolute input resistance {cite:p}`Furdan2026`, and the generalised assumption that all GABAergic subclasses share a dependence on a single channel fails within mouse itself {cite:p}`Liebergall2024`. Subtype identity is the level at which mouse data do not transfer; intrinsic biophysical mechanism is a level at which they may. Human-cell systems that bypass the inference are now available - grafted human pluripotent-stem-cell-derived medial ganglionic eminence cells develop almost exclusively (97%) into SST and PVALB subtypes {cite:p}`Bershteyn2025` - and early functional phenotypes in humanised knock-in models are measurable before any cell loss, with entorhinal parvalbumin interneurons hypoexcitable while neighbouring excitatory neurons are hyperexcitable at two months {cite:p}`Gonzalez2026`.

(sec-11-inventory)=

## An inventory of the disagreements

Ninety cross-study disagreements were recorded across the ten topic areas of this review, and {numref}`fig-sec11-conflict-atlas` classifies all of them by the axis on which the two studies differ and by status. Measurement modality and what was counted is the largest primary axis (37 of 90), followed by species, region and circuit context (21), model construct and species transfer (17), cohort composition, stage and comorbidity (8), and design rigor and statistical unit (7); 77 of the 90 span more than one axis, so the primary assignment marks emphasis rather than a partition. Forty-five are open and eleven unresolved, against twenty-three resolved by identifying the methodological difference and eleven partially resolved - which is what justifies treating the axes, rather than the diseases, as the organising variables. The last axis is the one least often visible in primary reports: neuron-number estimates for the same structure differ by about 15% between groups using the isotropic fractionator and often differ by more than 100% when the same stereological method is applied by different investigators to the same species and structure {cite:p}`HerculanoHouzel2015`; frozen sections shrink along the z-axis to roughly 25% of cut thickness with about 10% between-section variability, biasing optical-disector counts unless measured thickness is used {cite:p}`Carlo2011`; automatic image alignment in a physical-disector implementation gives a considerable economy of effort over manual alignment of tissue sections {cite:p}`Fabricius2017`; and between-animal variance accounted for 31.98% and 52.83% of total variance in excitatory and inhibitory synaptic charge, so small-cohort studies can report group differences that reflect animal sampling {cite:p}`Chen2018`. Two limits on the inventory must be stated plainly. The searches for disconfirming evidence were capped per query, so the inventory is a top-relevance sample rather than a census and the number of contradicted or unreplicated claims is bounded below, not estimated. The evidence behind this section is also unevenly verified: 45 of the 79 papers in its source literature were available as full text, and 25 of the 60 findings used here are recorded at abstract level only - a shortfall that is not confined to the older closed-access literature, since ten of those abstract-only records were published from 2018 onward and their quantitative claims could not be checked against a full text. The human cortical Huntington estimate discussed in {ref}`sec-huntingtons` is an unrefereed preprint, treated as provisional wherever it appears.

(sec-11-open-questions)=

## Open questions, ranked by what they would settle

Ranked by how much they would change the answer to the thesis question, six measurements are missing. First and most consequential: no study applying a marker-independent identification of human somatostatin interneurons - Sst transcript in situ hybridisation with a pan-GABAergic or nuclear-identity readout in the same tissue - to Alzheimer cortex was retrieved by these searches. Every reporter-based test of marker downregulation against cell death comes from rodent models, whether it dissociates the two {cite:p}`Voelkl2022` or finds the labelled population itself reduced {cite:p}`Drake2024`, while the human claim rests on immunoreactive counts {cite:p}`SaizSanchez2014, SanchezMejias2019`, and the clinical reading - degenerative endpoint or reversible expression state - turns on that distinction. Second, a somatostatin-selective causal manipulation inside a disease model rather than a healthy animal would test whether the circuit consequences described in {ref}`sec-circuit-consequences` follow from the deficit itself; the available causal manipulation targets a broader Dlx-expressing population {cite:p}`Matringhen2025`, and subclass-specific effects are not interchangeable, since a fourth interneuron subclass was spared in a model where parvalbumin, somatostatin and VIP cells were affected {cite:p}`Liebergall2024`. Third, a prospective cerebrospinal fluid somatostatin study reporting discrimination statistics rather than group means would decide the biomarker question left open in {ref}`sec-human-evidence`. Fourth, cell-type-resolved neprilysin measurement in human Alzheimer cortex would test the amyloid-degradation account disputed in {ref}`sec-alzheimers`, in tissue where the peptide may have degraded before assay {cite:p}`Salviati2025`. Fifth, the vulnerability and resilience cohorts need reconciling in the same donors with the same annotation, since they disagree about whether the somatostatin system is depleted in human Alzheimer cortex at all {cite:p}`SaizSanchez2014, Morgan2024a`, and the analytic-unit sensitivity of composite marker indices {cite:p}`Dienel2025` means the answer partly depends on how the comparison is framed. Sixth, the metabolic-load and calcium-handling accounts introduced in {ref}`sec-selective-vulnerability` need primary measurement in identified somatostatin cells; they are widely repeated, including in current syntheses {cite:p}`Okechukwu2026`, but no traceable primary demonstration was retrieved, and the same holds for the ranking that places somatostatin interneurons first among vulnerable interneuron classes - which the negative stereological results in amyloid models contradict {cite:p}`Futacsi2025, Sos2020`.

Four observations this section leans on are, on the present record, unreplicated and should be read as such: the single-region quantification of parvalbumin overlap in Sst-Cre presubiculum {cite:p}`Nassar2015`; the demonstration that a 3 h postmortem interval alone generates substantial neuronal differential expression {cite:p}`Olney2025`; the review that names the endogenous locus, Fgf14, disrupted by transgene insertion in a widely used tauopathy line, the primary observation being attributed there to an earlier study {cite:p}`Sahara2023`; and the finding that a GFP line used as a proxy for cortical somatostatin interneurons captures fewer than half of them {cite:p}`Zhou2019`. The Cre-reporter dissociation in a Huntington's model {cite:p}`Voelkl2022`, argued in {ref}`sec-huntingtons`, and the rare-variant account of somatostatin interneurons in cognitively resilient Alzheimer cases discussed in {ref}`sec-alzheimers` are also single reports, the latter an unrefereed preprint; both are load-bearing for the arguments they support and neither has been independently reproduced in the material retrieved here. Tool-level corrections compound the same way: specificity and coverage errors of the magnitude quantified above {cite:p}`MullerKomorowska2020, Zhou2019`, strain and background confounds {cite:p}`Foss2024, Bhaduri2018`, and construct-dosage differences spanning more than an order of magnitude {cite:p}`Guo2013, Sahara2023, Yeapuri2025` are each acknowledged individually in the primary literature and almost never applied jointly to a reported interneuron phenotype. What the inventory shows is not that the somatostatin findings are wrong, but that the field has been reading a measurement as a cell. {ref}`sec-conclusion` converts that inventory into an answer to the question this review set out with, and into the shortest list of experiments that would change it.
