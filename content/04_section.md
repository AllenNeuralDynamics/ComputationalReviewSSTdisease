# Alzheimer's Disease: SST Loss, Amyloid-β and Neprilysin, Tau, and Network Hyperexcitability

{ref}`sec-selective-vulnerability` established that reductions in somatostatin markers as large as those reported in disease also follow ageing and a wide range of insults. A fall in peptide measured in dissected tissue is therefore not by itself a statement about cells. Alzheimer's disease (AD) is where that ambiguity acquires a mechanistic stake, because somatostatin has been proposed to control the clearance of amyloid-β (Aβ) directly, through the peptidase neprilysin (NEP) {cite:p}`Saito2005, Nilsson2020, Watamura2021`. The receptor pharmacology and peptide processing that the axis depends on are set out in {ref}`sec-sst-identity`. What follows audits both halves of the proposition.

The cortical somatostatin deficit is the most reproducible observation in the AD evidence surveyed in this section: independent groups, four decades, four non-interconvertible measurement families, one consistent direction {cite:p}`Davies1981, Rossor1980, Arai1984, Mazurek1991, Consens2022a, Castanho2025b`.

Nearly everything built on top of it is contested:

- whether SST interneurons are lost or preserved
- whether somatostatin acts on amyloid through neprilysin or by interfering with aggregation
- whether SST cells are hyperactive or structurally degenerating under amyloid load
- whether tau preferentially burdens interneurons at all

(sec-04-the-deficit)=

## The deficit itself: what is reproducible and what is only directional

The founding observations are a set of regional radioimmunoassay (RIA) surveys from 1980 to 1991, and their agreement is directional and not quantitative.

{cite:t}`Davies1981` measured choline acetyltransferase (ChAT) activity and somatostatin-like immunoreactivity (SLI) in 8 brain regions from 12 normal individuals and 12 cases of presenile and senile dementia of the Alzheimer type (SDAT). ChAT was significantly lower in all 8 regions and SLI significantly reduced in 7 of the 8 — a near-global deficit across the regions sampled. {cite:t}`Rossor1980`, comparing 15 SDAT cases with 16 controls, found the fall in temporal cortex ChAT activity accompanied by a significant 47% reduction in immunoreactive somatostatin. {cite:t}`Arai1984` widened the panel to 21 regions in 7 histologically verified cases and 10 histologically normal controls, and recovered a focal result instead: SLI was significantly reduced in orbital cortex, hippocampus and putamen. {cite:t}`Mazurek1991` measured somatostatin and cholecystokinin in the same extracts from 14 histologically confirmed AD cases and 17 age-matched controls, and found somatostatin consistently decreased by 45–65%, against a cholecystokinin reduction of 24–38% in five of the 11 cortical areas examined.

Converging reports from the same era add four observations:

- a selective decrease in the basal nucleus of the amygdala, with no change in the nucleus of Meynert {cite:p}`Candy1985`
- a somatostatin reduction that scaled with neuritic plaque and tangle counts, while neuropeptide Y was unchanged in the same cortex {cite:p}`Dawbarn1986`
- morphological degeneration of somatostatin-immunoreactive perikarya, the surviving neurons distorted with short dendrites and truncated axons {cite:p}`ChanPalay1987`
- tangles located inside somatostatin-immunoreactive neurons {cite:p}`Roberts1985`

No reversal of direction appears anywhere in this series. But a sample weighted towards the most relevant reports is not a census of the disconfirming ones, so the agreement carries more weight than the absence of contradiction. The magnitude, the regional extent and the comparison tissue all do vary.

:::{admonition} Evidence Conflict
:class: warning
**Anatomical extent of the deficit beyond neocortex (unresolved).** {cite:t}`Beal1986a` reports no significant reductions in subcortical regions. {cite:t}`Candy1985` reports the opposite for one of them: somatostatin immunoreactivity was unchanged in the nucleus of Meynert in the SDAT group, but tissue punches taken from the amygdala revealed a selective decrease in the basal nucleus. The recorded axis of disagreement is measurement modality and what was counted — the two series sampled different subcortical nuclei — so the dispute concerns anatomical extent rather than direction. {cite:t}`Arai1984`, sampling 21 regions, adds a significant SLI reduction in putamen, which sits with the view that the deficit is not confined to neocortex without settling which nuclei carry it.
:::

The cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) series is a second, non-interchangeable construct, and it carries its own internal disagreement.

{cite:t}`Soininen1984` measured CSF in 59 AD/SDAT patients and 19 age-matched controls, and found both cholinesterase activities and SLI concentrations significantly reduced, with levels correlated with dementia severity. {cite:t}`Atack1988` measured somatostatin and neuropeptide Y in CSF from 26 healthy normal subjects, 27 patients with dementia of the Alzheimer type (DAT) and seven patients with DAT with extrapyramidal signs (EDAT). Concentrations were 17.5 ± 5.0 pg/mL (DAT) and 16.4 ± 5.0 pg/mL (EDAT) against 23.1 ± 8.2 pg/mL in age-matched controls — significantly reduced, but unrelated to dementia severity and not changing significantly during the progression of the disease. {cite:t}`Urakami1989` adds a stratification: CSF acetylcholinesterase and somatostatin were significantly lower in AD and in one senile-dementia subgroup, but not significantly different in two others.

{cite:t}`Cramer1985b` showed that what the assay measures is itself heterogeneous — at least four SLI peaks elute, two with apparent molecular weights of about 10,000 and 15,500, and the ratio of mature somatostatin to higher-molecular-weight SLI was increased in patients with dementia. {cite:t}`Duron2018` moved the fluid measurement into the biomarker era, finding CSF somatostatin independently associated with CSF Aβ1–42 (p = 0.01) in a memory-clinic cohort of 43.

A polyclonal RIA on a fluid that integrates the whole central nervous system is not the instrument that a regional tissue assay is, and the two should not be read as replications of each other. The CSF series is taken up as a series in {ref}`sec-human-evidence`.

:::{admonition} Evidence Conflict
:class: warning
**Does CSF somatostatin track dementia severity? (unresolved).** {cite:t}`Soininen1984` reports cholinesterase activities and SLI concentrations of cerebrospinal fluid significantly reduced in dementia patients relative to controls, with SLI levels correlated with the severity of dementia. {cite:t}`Gomez1986b` reports that SLI levels and AChE activities were not correlated with disease duration or with the dementia score. The recorded axis of disagreement is cohort composition, stage and comorbidity, and neither study reports the other's stratification. {cite:t}`Atack1988`, quoted above, falls on the same side as {cite:t}`Gomez1986b`.
:::

Transcriptomic and proteomic assays reproduce the direction on scales that cannot be converted into the RIA numbers.

Deconvolution of 1037 postmortem brain samples from six neocortical regions identified fewer SST inhibitory neurons as the strongest cell-type association with AD (β = −0.48, p_bonf = 8.98 × 10⁻⁹), narrowly ahead of intra-telencephalic excitatory neurons (β = −0.45, p_bonf = 4.32 × 10⁻⁷). Single-nucleus data especially support the SST association, in a single mega-analysis reported as preprint and journal article {cite:p}`Consens2022a, Consens2022b`. In the same cohort family, only SST relative cell-type proportions were negatively associated with each tau- and amyloid-related neuropathology, most strongly with neuritic plaques (p_FDR = 3.1 × 10⁻⁴), and positively associated with rates of cognitive decline (p_FDR = 3.9 × 10⁻⁶) {cite:p}`Consens2022b`.

Single-nucleus sequencing of entorhinal cortex places the signal inside inhibitory neurons as a class: *SST* expression was lower in AD, with log2FC = −0.52 against cognitively resilient donors and log2FC = −0.41 against controls, in the preprint and journal reports of one cohort {cite:p}`Castanho2025b, Castanho2025a`. Peptidomics of human cortex finds somatostatin-derived signalling proteoforms more abundant in AD-resilient than in AD-dementia brain {cite:p}`Morgan2024b`.

Cell counting gives a third family again: a 50% reduction in hippocampal SST-immunoreactive cell number (p = 0.0066, t = 3.640, df = 8), with preservation in the olfactory bulb {cite:p}`GonzalezRodriguez2021`. A pharmacological review reports, from a 2005 primary study, that SSTR4 and SSTR5 immunoreactivity is significantly decreased in AD frontal cortex {cite:p}`Sandoval2024`.

The subpopulation structure is finer than any of these summaries. Cortical SST28 neurons lacking neuropeptide Y and NADPH-diaphorase co-localisation in layers III and V are preferentially lost, and the decrease in the proportion of single-labelled SST neurons correlated with senile plaque counts (r = −0.89, P < 0.001) {cite:p}`Gaspar1989`. Somatostatin-immunoreactive profiles also share single senile plaques with substance P profiles {cite:p}`Armstrong1989`.

Four decades and four measurement families agree on sign. That is not a statement about magnitude. Three of the quantities on offer cannot be converted into one another:

- a percent change in tissue peptide concentration {cite:p}`Mazurek1991, Rossor1980`
- a standardized regression coefficient on deconvolved cell-type proportions {cite:p}`Consens2022b`
- a per-cell transcript log2 fold change {cite:p}`Castanho2025b`

The regional panels behind the early numbers run from near-global (SLI significantly reduced in 7 of the 8 regions sampled) to focal (orbital cortex, hippocampus and putamen, within a 21-region panel), and the two are not comparable proportions {cite:p}`Davies1981, Arai1984`. Two of the frequently quoted approximately 50% values are review syntheses reporting figures attributed to primary sources, and not independent measurements {cite:p}`Sandoval2024, Solarski2018`. {numref}`fig-sec4-deficit-magnitude-scales` shows the three modern assay families on separate axes for exactly this reason, and {numref}`fig-sec4-classic-postmortem-csf` shows the 1980–1988 series with each panel annotated by its diagnostic criterion.

(sec-04-vulnerable-or-resilient)=

## Vulnerable or resilient? The same data type, opposite conclusions

Two human transcriptomic studies of similar design reach opposite conclusions about the same cell class, and the comparator group is doing most of the work.

{cite:t}`Consens2022b` deconvolved bulk and single-nucleus cortical data against unselected non-AD controls, and reported SST interneuron proportions as the cell type most reduced in AD, with the reduction tracking plaque burden and cognitive decline. {cite:t}`Castanho2025b` sequenced entorhinal cortex and dorsolateral prefrontal cortex nuclei from 48 individuals spanning AD, cognitively resilient and control donors, and described inhibitory neurons overall as carrying resistance-associated expression profiles, with SST-positive inhibitory subtypes enriched for protective-variant expression. That same cohort simultaneously reports *SST* transcript itself as one of the most significantly downregulated genes in AD entorhinal inhibitory neurons {cite:p}`Castanho2025a`.

The two results are not arithmetically incompatible. A population can be transcriptionally preserved relative to donors matched for pathology and still carry less *SST* message per cell than a control. They are incompatible as summaries, and the peptidomic finding that somatostatin-derived proteoforms are more abundant in resilient than demented AD cortex sits on the resilience side of that line {cite:p}`Morgan2024b`. No study is known to reconcile the two using the same donors, annotation and comparator group; the single-nucleus cohorts that could adjudicate it are examined in {ref}`sec-human-evidence`.

:::{admonition} Evidence Conflict
:class: warning
**Most depleted cell type, or selectively preserved population? (unresolved).** {cite:t}`Consens2022b` reports SST interneuron relative proportions as the strongest AD-associated cell-type reduction in a mega-analysis of postmortem cortex; {cite:t}`Castanho2025b` describes SST-positive inhibitory neurons as selectively preserved and protective in a resilience-focused single-nucleus cohort. The recorded dimension of disagreement is design and statistical unit — deconvolved relative proportions across 1037 bulk samples versus per-nucleus differential expression in 48 individuals — compounded by comparator group (AD versus unselected controls, versus AD versus cognitively resilient donors at matched pathology). Both conclusions are currently drawn from similar human data types, which is why no quantitative statement about the magnitude of SST interneuron loss in AD is defensible.
:::

The animal-model literature adds a regional disagreement with the same structure.

{cite:t}`SanchezMejias2019` counted somatostatin-immunoreactive neurons in perirhinal cortex of APP/PS1 mice, and found a decrease of 55.99 ± 9.13% and 57.28 ± 10.96% in the deep layers of areas 36 and 35 at 6 months relative to 4 months. The transgenic-versus-control contrast at 6 months in that cohort is a dramatic decrease of 57% and 61% in the same two areas. {cite:t}`Ren2025`, tracing long-range inputs to prefrontal interneurons in an AD model at 9 months, found the number of Cre-reporter-labelled SOM-positive neurons in prelimbic cortex not reduced, while PV-positive neurons decreased by 16.67%. {cite:t}`Albuquerque2015` reported a significant decrease of SOM-immunoreactive cells in TgCRND8 hippocampus overall (p = 0.049) and in CA1/2 (p = 0.040), without an effect magnitude.

The populations counted are not the same quantity. Immunoreactivity can be lost through peptide downregulation in a surviving neuron while a Cre-driven reporter persists, so a reporter-labelled null does not refute an immunoreactivity loss. That methodological argument is this review's own, and the two studies supply only the measurements being compared: an immunoreactivity-based human count {cite:p}`GonzalezRodriguez2021` and a Cre-reporter-based mouse count {cite:p}`Ren2025`.

Model construct compounds the problem further. In hAPP transgenic hippocampus, at least 25% and up to 76% of somatostatin-expressing neurons across layers themselves express the hAPP transgene, so interneuron phenotypes in those lines are partly cell-autonomous and not purely a response to extrinsic amyloid {cite:p}`Hofling2019`. The construct-and-dosage explanation for divergent amyloid-model phenotypes is developed in {ref}`sec-conflicts-limitations`.

:::{admonition} Evidence Conflict
:class: warning
**Regional selectivity of SOM neuron decline in amyloid models (unresolved).** {cite:t}`SanchezMejias2019` reports a decline of more than half in SOM-positive neurons in deep perirhinal layers of APP/PS1 mice between 4 and 6 months; {cite:t}`Ren2025` finds no reduction in SOM-positive neuron number in prelimbic prefrontal cortex of 9-month-old AD model mice, where only PV-positive neurons decline. Whether this is regional selectivity or a general SST vulnerability cannot be settled from these two designs, which differ in model construct, age, region and labelling method.
:::

What is not in dispute is that SST cells sit inside the amyloid microenvironment. Three preparations place them there:

- intracellular Aβ accumulates progressively in SST- and CCK-expressing interneurons of *App* NL-F/NL-F hippocampus, with somatic colocalisation of 13 ± 4.0% for SST cells and 9 ± 3.7% for CCK cells, while calretinin cells are comparatively spared {cite:p}`Shi2019`
- multi-region spatial transcriptomics finds *Sst* among the GABAergic types significantly enriched within plaque spots across four regions {cite:p}`Bayaraa2025`
- region-resolved interneuron transcriptomics in 5XFAD mice selects the top differentially expressed genes in CA1 *Sst*-positive interneurons for validation, which the authors interpret as mirroring pathway alterations seen in human AD {cite:p}`Chen2025a`

Why that exposure should damage the cells has candidate cell-intrinsic answers. One is endoplasmic-reticulum stress generated by processing of the somatostatin precursor itself, with reduced *Perk* lowering ER stress markers in prefrontal SST neurons — shown in a chronic psychosocial-stress model with n = 4 per genotype, treatment and sex, and with no amyloid manipulation {cite:p}`Tomoda2022`. A major AD risk factor also remodels the same population's inputs: a few hours of sleep disruption alters SST interneuron dendritic spine density and spine size in a subregion-specific manner across hippocampus and neocortex {cite:p}`Raven2025`. {numref}`fig-sec4-sst-cell-change-regions` assembles the human and model cell-count results, with the human and mouse measurements kept in separate panels.

(sec-04-neprilysin-axis)=

## The somatostatin-neprilysin-amyloid axis and its direct refutation

The mechanistic hypothesis that makes the somatostatin deficit more than a correlate runs in three steps:

- somatostatin positively regulates neprilysin activity in neurons, and genetic somatostatin deficiency alters hippocampal neprilysin activity and localisation while raising Aβ42 in a manner the authors compared to presenilin mutations {cite:p}`Saito2005`
- neprilysin is transcriptionally enriched in the GABAergic compartment: among 480 protease and peptidase genes, *Mme* encoding neprilysin is one of only four enriched in cortical interneurons {cite:p}`Rossier2014`, which locates the transcript without measuring where enzyme activity resides, and endothelin-converting enzyme-2, a second major Aβ-degrading enzyme, is majorly expressed by somatostatin interneurons and active in isolated synaptosomes {cite:p}`PachecoQuinto2016`
- neprilysin carries a large share of brain Aβ catabolism, since deleting *Mme* in *App* knock-in mice more than doubles GuHCl-soluble Aβ40 and Aβ42, and combined neprilysin/insulin-degrading-enzyme (IDE) deficiency raises TS-soluble Aβ more than eight-fold, in a fraction the authors note accounts for only 1–5% of total Aβ42 {cite:p}`Morito2025`; the side-by-side knockout comparison assigns insoluble amyloidogenic Aβ to neprilysin and soluble Aβ to IDE {cite:p}`Sasaguri2021`

A post-transcriptional route into the same node has been described, with microRNA-181d-5p repressing neprilysin 3′-UTR activity, mRNA, protein and enzymatic activity while raising tau {cite:p}`Wang2026`.

The cellular assignment in the second step is contradicted within the same body of work. The knockout comparison that separates insoluble from soluble Aβ attributes neprilysin to secretory vesicles and the presynaptic membranes of excitatory neurons, with neuropeptides secreted from inhibitory neurons instead. The same work reports that neprilysin deficiency had no significant impact on somatostatin, substance P, cholecystokinin or neuropeptide Y levels {cite:p}`Sasaguri2021`. The chain is therefore coherent as a pathway while its cellular premise remains unsettled. Whether the cortex's principal Aβ-degrading capacity sits inside the very interneurons whose peptide is depleted depends on which of these localisation results holds.

The receptor arm supplies the strongest positive evidence and also the therapeutic rationale {cite:p}`Nilsson2020, Nilsson2025`:

- loss of SSTR1 and SSTR4 together lowers presynaptic neprilysin and raises hippocampal Aβ42 in *App* knock-in mice
- single *Sstr1* or *Sstr4* knockouts leave Aβ unaltered
- an SSTR1/SSTR4-selective agonist robustly increased hippocampal neprilysin expression, alongside a clear reduction in plaque load relative to the contralateral side

Those two reports carry the same SSTR1/SSTR4–neprilysin result, and one of them carries a preprint identifier. Whether they are two independent tests or one programme reported twice cannot be determined from the reports themselves, so the receptor arm may rest on fewer experiments than the citation count suggests.

Downstream of the receptor, somatostatin control of neprilysin has been placed on a SUR1/Kir6.2 pathway: *Abcc8* knockout significantly decreased hippocampal neprilysin activity, with a significant increase of both Aβ40 and Aβ42 {cite:p}`Watamura2021`. α-Endosulfine has been identified as a negative regulator acting downstream, its genetic loss enhancing neprilysin activity and decreasing Aβ deposition {cite:p}`Watamura2020`.

Pharmacological agonism reproduces the transcript signature. The SSTR4 agonist NNC 26-9100 increased cortical expression of *Sstr4* 4.9-fold, neprilysin 9.3-fold, insulin-degrading enzyme 14.8-fold and catalase 3.6-fold at 24 h {cite:p}`Sandoval2019`. The same compound improved learning while lowering soluble Aβ42 oligomers through a phosphoramidon-sensitive metalloproteinase mechanism {cite:p}`Sandoval2013`. A different SSTR4 agonist, L-803087, applied to differentiated SH-SY5Y cells, raised Ser522-CRMP2 phosphorylation in a concentration-dependent manner, alone and in combination with Aβ1–42. In that study the protective arm is somatostatin-mediated inhibition of Ser522-CRMP2 phosphorylation, and the agonist-driven increase is read as a consequence of receptor internalisation and not as the means by which Aβ cytotoxicity is counteracted {cite:p}`Paik2021`.

Brain-penetrant protein engineering targets the same enzyme from the other side:

- a somatostatin fusion protein raised hippocampal neprilysin levels, with the membrane-fraction increase correlating with the fall in Aβ42 {cite:p}`Rofo2021`
- a somatostatin-based therapeutic that activates neprilysin reduced both oligomeric and larger Aβ aggregates {cite:p}`Metzendorf2025`
- directed evolution produced a neprilysin variant with 20-fold increased catalytic efficiency on Aβ1–40 and up to 3.2 × 10³-fold reduced cleavage of off-target peptides {cite:p}`Webster2014`
- a blood–brain-barrier-penetrating fusion construct showed Aβ40 degradation that depended strongly on the Aβ-to-enzyme ratio {cite:p}`Rofo2022`

Reciprocally, intracortical Aβ25–35 lowers both somatostatin and neprilysin expression in rat temporal cortex, and minocycline prevents both reductions {cite:p}`BurgosRamos2009`.

Against that coherence stands one in vivo test on an amyloidosis background, and it is negative on the mechanism. Crossing *Sst*-null mice to the *App* NL-F/NL-F knock-in, {cite:t}`Williams2023` found that neither whole-brain neprilysin transcript nor protein nor activity levels were affected by *Sst* genotype, yet *Sst* ablation still produced significantly stronger cortical Aβ deposition at 15 months. Cortical plaque density rose 3.7-fold, 4.6-fold and 4.5-fold between 12 and 15 months in the three *Sst* genotypes, and the paralog *Cort* increased by approximately 35% in *Sst*-deficient mice without preventing the plaque increase — the journal and preprint reports of that one cross {cite:p}`Williams2023, Williams2022`.

The authors' interpretation is that endogenous somatostatin slows deposition by interfering with Aβ aggregation directly, and not by inducing neprilysin. Independent biophysical work supports a direct interaction:

- somatostatin-14 binds selectively to the β-barrel pore-forming Aβ(1–42) tetramer with a KD of approximately 40 µM and 2:1 stoichiometry, without binding monomeric Aβ(1–42) {cite:p}`Puig2020`
- elevated somatostatin and Aβ may form mixed oligomeric assemblies, with a specific somatostatin residue required for the effect on fibrillization kinetics {cite:p}`Solarski2018`
- the clinical analogues octreotide and lanreotide protected hippocampal cells from Aβ42 toxicity (cell viability 83.8% and 81%) more effectively than native somatostatin {cite:p}`Pham2025`

A third, non-enzymatic route is also on the table, with somatostatin acting on microglia through SSTR2 to enhance phagocytosis, and somatostatin overexpression reducing microglial density and plaque burden while improving hippocampus-dependent memory {cite:p}`Jung2026`.

:::{admonition} Evidence Conflict
:class: warning
**Does somatostatin control brain Aβ through neprilysin? (unresolved; the most consequential disagreement in this review).** {cite:t}`Saito2005` reports that somatostatin selectively upregulates neprilysin activity among tested effectors and that genetic somatostatin deficiency alters hippocampal neprilysin activity and localisation while increasing Aβ42. {cite:t}`Williams2023` reports no effect of *Sst* ablation on whole-brain neprilysin transcript, protein or activity in an *App* knock-in background, with cortical plaque density nonetheless significantly increased, and attributes the effect to direct interference with Aβ aggregation. The recorded axis of disagreement is measurement modality and what was counted — hippocampal enzyme activity in a wild-type-background knockout versus whole-brain transcript, protein and activity in an amyloid knock-in cross. The recorded disagreement in the receptor arm sets {cite:t}`Nilsson2020`, which reports that combined SSTR1/SSTR4 loss lowers presynaptic neprilysin and raises hippocampal Aβ42, against {cite:t}`Williams2022`, which reports unchanged brain neprilysin after ligand knockout on the same *App* knock-in background; {cite:t}`Williams2022` is the preprint of {cite:t}`Williams2023`, so this is the same ligand-knockout experiment appearing a second time rather than an additional one. Both sets of results are currently cited as if they agreed. No study measuring neprilysin protein and activity, Aβ species and plaque load side by side in ligand-knockout and SSTR1/SSTR4 receptor-knockout mice on the same *App* knock-in background was retrieved by these searches.
:::

Two further cautions belong with this axis.

First, the claim that neprilysin is regulated by the neuropeptide somatostatin is repeated across primary papers and reviews, but no original demonstration in human tissue is known: the primary evidence is rodent or cell culture throughout. No human study is known to quantify neprilysin protein or activity specifically within SST or PV interneurons in AD versus control cortex — the missing load-bearing experiment, if that degrading capacity is indeed concentrated in the interneurons that lose their peptide.

Second, the therapeutic programme described in {ref}`sec-therapeutics` inherits the neprilysin premise from this disputed chain. That programme now runs to thirty-three SSTR4-selective compounds with sub-100 nM binding affinity, five of them sub-nanomolar with more than 300-fold selectivity over other subtypes {cite:p}`Crider2025`, together with structural refinement of SSTR4 agonist contacts {cite:p}`Slater2025`. It also inherits a negative human precedent: octreotide produced no clinically significant cognitive improvement in Alzheimer dementia, and a generalized decrease in glucose metabolism on positron emission tomography in 6 patients {cite:p}`Mouradian1991`. {numref}`fig-sec4-neprilysin-axis` sets out the quantities that are actually measured in this axis, and makes clear that they do not form a single causal chain.

(sec-04-tau-tropism)=

## Tau, SST interneurons, and cell-class tropism

Whether pathological tau preferentially burdens inhibitory neurons is contested along a seeded-versus-spontaneous axis.

Inoculating mouse hippocampus with extracellular vesicles from AD brain, {cite:t}`Ruan2020` found that 64% of AT8-positive cells were GAD67-positive interneurons, against 23% GluR2/3-positive excitatory mossy cells. Interneurons receiving the transferred tau fired fewer action potentials at +100 pA (P = 0.0434) and +130 pA (P = 0.0445) than those from tau-knockout-vesicle controls.

Human postmortem and PS19 transgenic material point the other way on aggregation. {cite:t}`Broekaart2025` reports tau aggregating predominantly in excitatory neurons, while finding inhibitory neurons far more transcriptionally perturbed in vulnerable regions — 1733 differentially expressed genes in prefrontal inhibitory neurons against 223 in excitatory neurons. Human induced pluripotent stem cell-derived subtypes seeded with sporadic AD brain tau sit with the interneuron-vulnerability side, cortical inhibitory neurons consistently displaying the most MC1-positive insoluble tau aggregation across donor lines {cite:p}`Weidling2025`. A chaperone-based account identifies a tau homeostasis signature instead. BAG3 overexpression significantly attenuated tau accumulation in excitatory neurons (P < 0.01), with only a non-significant trend in inhibitory neurons (P = 0.098), attributed to the low level of tau aggregate in inhibitory cells in the first place {cite:p}`Fu2018`.

Cross-dataset modelling of regional tau burden recovers excitatory CA1-prosubiculum neuron density as the dominant correlate. *Sst*-positive interneurons correlate positively with tau across datasets while *Pvalb*-positive interneurons show the opposite, and a positive association for both together appears only in the unseeded combined amyloid/tau model {cite:p}`Torok2025`.

:::{admonition} Evidence Conflict
:class: warning
**Cell-class tropism of pathological tau (unresolved).** {cite:t}`Ruan2020` finds the majority of AT8-positive cells in inoculated mouse hippocampus to be GAD67-positive interneurons; {cite:t}`Broekaart2025` finds tau accumulation predominantly in excitatory neurons in human cortex and PS19 mice. The recorded axis is species, region and circuit context, confounded by seeded mouse inoculation versus spontaneous human and transgenic pathology, and by region — hippocampus versus prefrontal cortex. Neither design can be converted into the other, and the disagreement is about which cells carry aggregate, not about whether inhibitory circuits are affected.
:::

Tau-bearing dystrophic survivors are a phenotype distinct from cell loss, and the earliest human material already described them. Surviving somatostatin neurons are distorted with short dendrites and truncated axons, with loss most severe where tangle and plaque indices are highest {cite:p}`ChanPalay1987`. Cortical somatostatin-immunoreactive perikarya show morphological changes consistent with degeneration {cite:p}`Roberts1985`.

The TgF344-AD rat reproduces this directly, with dystrophic SST neurons making up more than 40% of the hippocampal SST population at 15 months (F(2,6) = 72.61, P < 0.0001) while parvalbumin neuroplasticity compensates {cite:p}`Morrone2022`. The human pathological background against which to read it is a phospho-tau-positive neuron density that rises from 3.3 ± 5.8 AT8-positive neurons/mm² in non-demented cases to 14.2 ± 18.2 in Alzheimer's dementia {cite:p}`MerinoSerrais2025`.

Tau also degrades the excitatory drive onto inhibitory cells. In P301S hippocampus, AMPA receptor density falls at synaptic and extrasynaptic sites of both pyramidal cells and interneurons {cite:p}`AlfaroRuiz2022b`. Synaptic NMDA receptors on CA1 interneurons are reduced in a layer-specific pattern, while extrasynaptic GluN1 increases in stratum radiatum interneuron dendrites {cite:p}`AlfaroRuiz2022a`.

Three further consequences follow in tauopathy models:

- interneuron subtypes are lost early and progressively in the same line, with aberrantly increased LTP as the functional correlate {cite:p}`Bold2022`
- GABA-related gene expression falls in tauopathy prefrontal cortex, and epigenetic activation of *Gad1* restores inhibitory currents and spatial memory {cite:p}`Wan2025`
- positive allosteric modulation of α5-GABA-A receptors, a major postsynaptic target of dendritic inhibition, mitigated spine loss and reversed working-memory deficits in PS19 mice without altering phospho-tau levels {cite:p}`Dharavath2024`

Tau is not simply permissive for hyperexcitability either. Deleting *Mapt* changes the electrographic character of induced epileptogenesis instead of abolishing it: 10 of 17 (58.82%) tau-null animals progressed from status epilepticus to spontaneous recurrent seizures, against 38 of 42 (90.47%) wild-type mice {cite:p}`Moseley2025`.

(sec-04-hyperexcitability)=

## Network hyperexcitability: how much is biology and how much is recording time

Any statement about how common epileptiform activity is in AD is a statement about the recording protocol.

On overnight video-EEG with magnetoencephalography, subclinical epileptiform activity was detected in 42.4% of 33 AD patients without a seizure history, against 10.5% of 19 age-matched controls (p = 0.02), and it was associated with faster cognitive decline {cite:p}`Vossel2016`. A narrative review reports, from a cited study of 1617 unselected dementia patients on routine short EEG, epileptiform discharges in 42 subjects (3%), 60% of whom had no clinical seizures {cite:p}`Chen2024`. Pushing the electrode closer detects activity that scalp recording misses. In two index patients with no clinical or scalp-EEG evidence of epilepsy, foramen ovale recordings revealed clinically silent hippocampal seizures and spikes concentrated during sleep — a demonstration of detectability and not a prevalence estimate {cite:p}`Lam2017`. A frequently quoted literature range of 42%–64% for overt seizures or subclinical epileptic activity sits inside a mouse tau-model paper and not a clinical cohort {cite:p}`Gao2026`.

Detection criteria are themselves under question. In 30 patients with early-stage AD without a history of epilepsy, questionnaire-positive epilepsy symptoms were more common among patients with any suspicious EEG features (4/7, 57%) than among those without (4/23, 17%; p = 0.037) — a subgroup split within the patient arm. The cohort-level discharge rate, 3 patients (10%) against 1 control (3.33%), did not reach significance (p = 0.612), and the authors conclude that current morphological criteria may exclude true epileptiform activity in this population {cite:p}`BSzabo2026`.

These prevalence estimates come from small, heterogeneous cohorts with different modalities and no shared detection criteria, and they should not be pooled into a single figure for how common subclinical seizures are in AD. {numref}`fig-sec4-epileptiform-rates` groups them by detection protocol instead.

The surrounding human readouts are equally heterogeneous and equally small, and {numref}`fig-sec4-human-hyperexcitability-markers` keeps the two quantified ones on separate axes. Theta–gamma phase-amplitude coupling in left parahippocampal cortex was reduced in AD, with a cohort-level normalized value of −0.235 in 50 biomarker-positive patients against 0.312 in 35 cognitively unimpaired controls (P = 0.037) {cite:p}`Prabhu2024`. High-frequency oscillations above 250 Hz and epileptiform discharges are detectable on scalp EEG in Down syndrome with AD, where 57% (8/14) of participants showed epileptiform activity and HFO-positive channels were regionally distributed by symptomatic status (p = 0.003) {cite:p}`Lisgaras2025`. The inference also runs backwards: 17% of a prospective cohort with late-onset unexplained epilepsy met criteria for mild cognitive impairment (MCI) within five years of seizure onset {cite:p}`Sarkis2024`.

Whether suppressing the activity helps is still unanswered in humans. A randomized crossover trial of levetiracetam in AD patients who had never had a seizure recruited 8 of a planned 30 participants before being halted, establishing tolerability but not efficacy {cite:p}`Sen2024`. None of these human measurements identifies a cell type; the attribution of human network hyperexcitability to SST or PV interneuron dysfunction rests entirely on mouse models.

The mechanistic evidence that connects inhibition to this phenotype is almost entirely about parvalbumin cells.

hAPP transgenic mice show spontaneous nonconvulsive seizure activity in cortical and hippocampal networks, with GABAergic sprouting and enhanced synaptic inhibition in dentate gyrus {cite:p}`Palop2007`. Restoring Nav1.1 in those mice increased gamma activity and reduced epileptiform discharges, with hAPPJ20/Nav1.1 mice showing fewer spikes than hAPPJ20 mice during low-intensity gamma activity {cite:p}`Verret2012`.

Three further manipulations fill in the same picture:

- levetiracetam suppressed the aberrant network activity and reversed hippocampal remodelling, synaptic and cognitive deficits in the same model family {cite:p}`Sanchez2012`
- interictal spikes in Tg2576 mice are state-dependent and cholinergically gated, with rates significantly higher in REM than wakefulness in n = 5 mice {cite:p}`Scharfman2025`
- pharmacological disruption of the GSK3β/Nav1.6 complex dose-dependently suppressed epileptiform spiking during non-rapid-eye-movement (NREM) sleep, from 155.8 ± 11.79 spikes/h in vehicle to 90.46 ± 16.84 at 50 mg/kg (p = 0.0011) and 58.24 ± 11.16 at 75 mg/kg, with n = 3 animals per dose {cite:p}`Baumgartner2025`

The structural substrate is also compartment-specific. Aβ overexpression in aged animals was accompanied by a 58% decrease in synapse density at CA1 basal dendrites relative to wild-type littermates {cite:p}`Ray2024`. Laminar neural-mass modelling shows that progressively weakening fast-interneuron inhibition onto pyramidal cells reproduces the hallmark EEG slowing and hyperexcitability signatures of AD progression {cite:p}`SanchezTodo2026`. How dendritic inhibition loss produces these oscillatory phenotypes is developed in {ref}`sec-circuit-consequences`.

:::{admonition} Evidence Conflict
:class: warning
**Is reduced Nav1.1 the mechanism of interneuron failure? (resolved by method, to the extent it is resolved).** {cite:t}`Verret2012` attributes PV interneuron failure and network hypersynchrony in an amyloid model to reduced Nav1.1, and rescues discharges by restoring it. {cite:t}`Kudo2023` finds fast-spiking interneurons selectively hypoactive in the rTg4510 tauopathy model, with excitatory neuron activity unchanged at 5–6 months, and states explicitly that the dysfunction cannot be explained by tau accumulation in interneurons or by altered Nav1.1 and Kv3.1b protein levels. The recorded axis is model construct and proteinopathy — hAPPJ20 amyloid versus rTg4510 tau — so the two results may both hold in their own systems; what they jointly exclude is a single channel-level account of inhibitory failure across proteinopathies. The rTg4510 exclusion of Nav1.1 and Kv3.1b has not been independently reproduced in the work retrieved by these searches.
:::

The step that this section cannot supply is the SST-specific one. No study is known to test whether restoring SST interneuron output alone normalises epileptiform activity, and the one available selective-loss manipulation argues against sufficiency. In otherwise healthy mice carrying no amyloid or tau pathology, conditional deletion of *c-Maf* from cortical SST interneurons reduced their excitatory synaptic drive and density. Yet *Mafb* conditional knockouts with reduced SST interneuron density did not exhibit epileptiform activity {cite:p}`Leroux2026`.

The claim that SST interneuron loss causes network hypersynchrony in AD is therefore widely asserted but not demonstrated by the selective-loss model that exists. The calcium-buffering route to tau-driven hyperexcitability puts the proximate defect outside the SST population altogether: calbindin-D28k is downregulated in hippocampal excitatory neurons, and adeno-associated-virus (AAV)-mediated restoration reduces hyperexcitability and microgliosis {cite:p}`Gao2026`.

(sec-04-hyperactive-or-degenerating)=

## Hyperactive or degenerating? SST interneuron state under amyloid

Three readouts of the SST population under amyloid point in three directions, and the discrepancy is largely a matter of what was measured and under what exposure.

In vivo two-photon calcium imaging of genetically labelled interneurons in APP/PS1 cortex found SOM interneuron event rates 2.7-fold higher than in wild-type, with SOM hyperactivity greatest near plaques, while PV interneurons in the same model showed a 1.8-fold decrease {cite:p}`Algamal2022b, Algamal2022a`. Ultrastructural analysis of *App* NL-F knock-in hippocampus found the opposite picture for the same class. SOM-positive dystrophic neurites sat at 89% of plaques (n = 45, 2 mice) and CB1R-positive dystrophic neurites at 93% (n = 75, 2 mice), whereas only 4% (n = 93, 2 mice) carried PV-positive ones {cite:p}`Sos2020`.

Acute exposure to soluble oligomers reverses the sign again. Soluble Aβ42 oligomers at 250 nM significantly reduced nicotine-induced activity in SST-positive interneurons (1.000 ± 0.391 to 0.600 ± 0.237 ΔF/F₀, p = 0.0002) and in PV-positive interneurons (1.000 ± 0.772 to 0.448 ± 0.233 ΔF/F₀, p = 0.0038) {cite:p}`Lee2026`. Co-activation of α7 and α4β2 nicotinic receptors reversed the deficit.

Somatic calcium activity and axonal integrity are different quantities, and chronic fibrillar plaque exposure in awake cortex is a different exposure from acute soluble-oligomer application to hippocampal neurons. Reading these as a contradiction about "SST function in amyloid" collapses both distinctions, which is why {numref}`fig-sec4-sst-activity-direction` separates the chronic and acute exposures.

What is missing is the within-animal comparison: spontaneous and cholinergically driven SST activity across the soluble-oligomer and plaque-bearing stages of the same model. No study is known to pair cell counts with physiological recording in the same animals or the same human tissue, which is why structural-loss and activity studies are currently set against each other with no shared preparation.

:::{admonition} Evidence Conflict
:class: warning
**Hyperactive, degenerating, or suppressed? (partially resolved for the first pair, open for the second).** {cite:t}`Algamal2022a` reports a 2.7-fold increase in SOM interneuron calcium event rates in an APP model, while {cite:t}`Sos2020` reports SOM-positive dystrophic neurites at 89% of plaques with near-complete sparing of PV terminals — functional hyperactivity against ultrastructural degeneration of the same population, reconcilable in principle because somatic calcium activity and terminal degeneration are different measurements. Separately, {cite:t}`Algamal2022b`'s chronic plaque-bearing hyperactivity stands against {cite:t}`Lee2026`'s suppression of nicotine-evoked activity in SST-positive interneurons after acute soluble oligomer exposure; the recorded axis is model construct and species transfer, and the therapeutic polarity — silence SST cells or boost them — cannot be settled from the present evidence. {cite:t}`Algamal2022a` and {cite:t}`Algamal2022b` are the journal and preprint reports of one imaging study, so the same result stands on both sides of this layout.
:::

Functional manipulations give the SST population a specific and partly separable role in the oscillatory phenotype.

Optogenetic activation of SST-positive but not PV-positive interneurons fully restored the peak power of theta oscillations and resynchronized theta spike phases in amyloid-oligomer-injected hippocampus, with PV activation restoring gamma power instead and the two deficits traced to dissociable presynaptic mechanisms {cite:p}`Chung2018, Chung2020`. Those are the preprint and published versions of the same experiments. The synaptic locus is not the SST cell's own input or output: oligomers had no effect on pyramidal-to-SST or SST-to-pyramidal synapses, but specifically disrupted SST interneuron-mediated disinhibition, impairing theta-nested gamma oscillation-induced spike-timing-dependent potentiation {cite:p}`Park2020`.

Supplying new inhibitory cells, instead of modulating the surviving ones, also restores function. Transplanting human iPSC-derived medial-ganglionic-eminence inhibitory neurons into 5xFAD hippocampus raised the novel-object discrimination index from 0.36 to 0.62 (p = 0.0017) against a wild-type value of 0.55, and increased novel-arm entries by 24.4% (p = 0.0198) {cite:p}`Zhang2026a`. Both gains appeared at three months post-transplant, with no significant improvement at two months. That graft is a mixed GABAergic population scored on a behavioural endpoint, so the benefit cannot be attributed to SST cells.

The gamma-entrainment programme occupies the same circuit space from the PV side:

- driving fast-spiking PV interneurons at 40 Hz, and non-invasive 40 Hz flicker, reduced Aβ1–40 and Aβ1–42 and mitigated plaque load {cite:p}`Iaccarino2016`
- auditory and audiovisual stimulation extended the effect to hippocampus and to phosphorylated tau in a P301S model {cite:p}`Martorell2019`
- multisensory 40 Hz stimulation increased cortical CSF tracer influx and meningeal lymphatic vessel diameter {cite:p}`Murdock2024`
- 40 Hz flicker raised CA3–CA1 slow-gamma phase coordination by 184% for pyramidal cells and 130% for interneurons during virtual navigation {cite:p}`Paulson2025`

:::{admonition} Evidence Conflict
:class: warning
**Why does 40 Hz stimulation help? (unresolved; carried forward).** {cite:t}`Iaccarino2016` attributes the benefit to frequency-specific entrainment that lowers Aβ levels and plaque load. {cite:t}`Aguilera2025` reports that two weeks of 40 Hz visual stimulation in 4-month-old *App*/*MAPT* double knock-in mice — an age at which the same study confirms no significant plaque deposition — restores impaired global EEG network dynamics to wild-type-comparable levels and rescues object-in-place memory, and proposes a non-amyloid mechanism that is not necessarily 40 Hz-specific. The disagreement is about mechanism rather than about whether the intervention works, and is taken up with the rest of the stimulation literature in {ref}`sec-therapeutics`.

(sec-04-what-the-evidence-supports)=

## What the AD evidence can and cannot support

The audited position is narrower than the literature's summary of itself. A reduction in the somatostatin signal in AD cortex is established in direction across the record surveyed here, reproduced by independent groups across four decades in four measurement families:

- tissue radioimmunoassay {cite:p}`Davies1981, Rossor1980, Arai1984, Mazurek1991`
- cerebrospinal fluid assay {cite:p}`Soininen1984, Atack1988, Urakami1989`
- cortical and hippocampal cell counting {cite:p}`Gaspar1989, GonzalezRodriguez2021`
- transcriptomic deconvolution and single-nucleus sequencing {cite:p}`Consens2022a, Consens2022b, Castanho2025b`

Nothing beyond direction survives the same scrutiny. The magnitudes are measured on non-interconvertible scales, and the regional panels behind the early cohorts differ from near-global to focal. The two single-nucleus and deconvolution cohorts frame the cells differently, and not oppositely. {cite:t}`Consens2022b` reports the SST-annotated inhibitory population proportionally depleted in AD, while {cite:t}`Castanho2025b` describes SST interneurons as a vulnerable population that is maintained in cognitively resilient individuals — a difference of cohort framing, not of direction.

The causal mechanism most often attached to the deficit is credible, with a receptor-level and a downstream-effector arm {cite:p}`Nilsson2020, Nilsson2025, Watamura2021, Watamura2020`. It also has a direct in vivo refutation on the record, from the one amyloid knock-in cross that tested it {cite:p}`Williams2023, Williams2022`. No human study is known to manipulate or longitudinally track SST interneuron number alongside amyloid load, so the causal direction rests entirely on rodent *Sst* knockout and agonist work. That gap matters, because the therapeutic rationale for SST-receptor agonists assumes that the rodent ordering transfers to humans.

Several claims in this section carry a replication status that their circulation does not reflect, and they are flagged here instead of being smoothed. The original result that somatostatin regulates brain Aβ42 by upregulating neprilysin activity {cite:p}`Saito2005` has not been independently reproduced in a knock-in amyloid model, and the single in vivo test on that background is negative on the enzyme {cite:p}`Williams2023`. Five further observations stand alone in the material assembled here:

- the mixed somatostatin–Aβ oligomeric assembly with a residue-specific effect on fibrillization kinetics {cite:p}`Solarski2018`
- the brain-penetrant somatostatin fusion protein that raises hippocampal neprilysin and lowers Aβ42 {cite:p}`Rofo2021`
- the endoplasmic-reticulum-stress origin of SST neuron vulnerability {cite:p}`Tomoda2022`
- the exclusion of Nav1.1 and Kv3.1b as the cause of fast-spiking interneuron hypoactivity in rTg4510 {cite:p}`Kudo2023`
- the superior protection of octreotide and lanreotide over native somatostatin against Aβ42 toxicity {cite:p}`Pham2025`

Three further limits are structural and not evidential. The evidence base behind this section came from one dominant bibliographic source with no citation chasing, so every absence stated above is an absence in that base and not in the literature. Full text could be read for 61 of 112 papers on Alzheimer's disease covered here. The shortfall falls on exactly the pre-2000 closed-access postmortem and cerebrospinal fluid series that carries the foundational claim, so the regional and methodological detail behind those cohorts rests on abstracts. The hunt for disconfirming results was capped, so the disagreements presented here are a top-relevance sample and not a census.

Eleven of the works cited in this section carry preprint or preprint-server identifiers instead of journal ones, several of them load-bearing:

- both parties to the receptor-arm neprilysin disagreement {cite:p}`Nilsson2020, Williams2022`
- the only SST-specific oscillatory rescue {cite:p}`Chung2018`
- the soluble-versus-insoluble Aβ assignment {cite:p}`Sasaguri2021`
- the α-endosulfine regulator {cite:p}`Watamura2020`
- the α5-GABA-A result {cite:p}`Dharavath2024`
- the peptidomic resilience result {cite:p}`Morgan2024b`
- the microRNA route to neprilysin {cite:p}`Wang2026`
- the SOM hyperactivity imaging {cite:p}`Algamal2022b`
- the preprint halves of the two human transcriptomic cohorts {cite:p}`Consens2022a, Castanho2025a`

AD therefore supplies the template that the rest of this review tests: a reproducible peptide deficit, a contested cellular interpretation, and a mechanistic chain whose therapeutic consequences are being drawn before its central step has been replicated. {ref}`sec-parkinsons-lewy-body` asks whether the same pattern appears in a different proteinopathy, and finds that the cortical deficit reported there may be imported from Alzheimer co-pathology instead of being generated by α-synuclein.
