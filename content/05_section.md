# Parkinson's Disease and Lewy Body Dementias

The cortical somatostatin deficit established in {ref}`sec-alzheimers` has a contested cellular interpretation, and Lewy body disease provides the natural test of whether that deficit is specific to Alzheimer-type pathology.

(sec-05-cortical-copathology)=

## Cortical somatostatin is conditional on Alzheimer-type co-pathology

The post-mortem record answers that the deficit tracks dementia; whether it additionally requires Alzheimer-type pathology cannot be settled from these series, because the two that report reductions selected cases carrying that pathology {cite:p}`Beal1986d, FlintBeal1988` and the Lewy-body-type dementia series do not report co-pathology status {cite:p}`Whitford1988, Leake1991`; the occipital series is the exception, staining for α-synuclein, Aβ and hyperphosphorylated tau and excluding cases with high coexisting Alzheimer pathology {cite:p}`Khundakar2016`. {cite:t}`Beal1986d` measured somatostatin-like immunoreactivity (SLI) by radioimmunoassay in post-mortem tissue from 15 control patients, 7 non-demented parkinsonian patients and 7 demented parkinsonian patients who had Alzheimer-type cortical pathology: the non-demented cases had normal SLI concentrations in cerebral cortex, hippocampus, amygdala, putamen, caudate or globus pallidus, while the demented cases with Alzheimer-type pathology showed reductions of approximately 40% in frontal (Brodmann area 6) and temporal (Brodmann area 21) cortex. {cite:t}`FlintBeal1988`, sampling 12 control patients and 13 demented parkinsonian patients with Alzheimer-type cortical pathology across twenty-two cortical regions, found significant SLI reductions in 17 regions, typically 50-60%, against neuropeptide Y (NPY) reductions of 20-30% in nine regions — a dissociation between two peptides that are co-expressed in the same cortical interneuron class, as set out in {ref}`sec-sst-identity`. The 1988 series shares its first author, and one further author, with the 1986 one and extends it to a larger region set, so the two are best read as a within-group replication rather than as two independent confirmations; whether they draw on shared case material is not stated in the retrieved text. {cite:t}`Leake1991` separated the syndromes directly, reporting that somatostatin was reduced only in temporal cortex in senile dementia of the Lewy body type and in senile dementia of the Alzheimer type, and unaltered in Parkinson's disease (PD) without dementia. The pattern is therefore indexed by dementia and co-pathology, not by a parkinsonian diagnosis, and this is the reading that {cite:t}`Leake1993` and {cite:t}`Beal1990` draw from the same literature, with the caveat that both are secondary accounts rather than new measurements. An older series reported decreased cortical SLI in all six cases of pathologically confirmed diffuse Lewy body disease alongside profound neocortical choline acetyltransferase loss {cite:p}`Dickson1987`; whether those cases also carried Alzheimer-type cortical pathology is the variable that determines how much weight the result can bear.

Two observations constrain the generality of the deficit. The modern dementia with Lewy bodies (DLB) series of {cite:t}`Khundakar2016` measured somatostatin by ELISA in primary visual cortex and found it unchanged in DLB (p = 0.40, uncorrected) and in AD (p = 0.09, uncorrected), while neuropeptide Y was increased by approximately 20% in AD only (p = 0.027, uncorrected); {cite:t}`Leake1991` reports reduction only in temporal cortex, which implies but does not state an occipital null. Region, not diagnosis, separates these nulls from the temporal and frontal reductions; {numref}`fig-sec5-cortical-somatostatin-conditional-on-copathology` arranges every series along the cognitive and neuropathological axis and keeps the magnitude and direction-only entries on separate panels. The second constraint is the absence of cell counts: every primary series above measures bulk peptide, the only morphological statement in this record being the qualitative one relayed by {cite:t}`Beal1990`, that somatostatin neurons in both illnesses are markedly dystrophic and may be reduced in number; and no study quantifying somatostatin-immunoreactive neuron numbers stereologically in DLB or Parkinson's disease dementia (PDD) neocortex was retrieved by these searches, nor any series of pure Lewy body disease in which Alzheimer-type pathology was excluded or covaried. Reduced peptide and reduced cell number are therefore not separable here, which is the central interpretive ambiguity of the cortical literature and the reason this section speaks of reduced somatostatin-like immunoreactivity rather than of interneuron loss.

:::{admonition} Evidence Conflict
:class: warning
{cite:t}`FlintBeal1988` report SLI reductions of typically 50-60% across 17 of twenty-two cortical regions in demented parkinsonian patients, whereas {cite:t}`Whitford1988` measured both SLI and high-affinity [3H]somatostatin binding sites in frontal (Brodmann area 9) and temporal (Brodmann area 21) cortex in presenile and senile Alzheimer's disease and in mentally normal and cognitively impaired cases of Parkinson's disease, and found no significant changes between any of the disease groups — and the authors suggest that somatostatinergic involvement is not a consistent or primary neurochemical feature of these diseases. The disagreement is not one of assay: both are radioimmunoassay series of the same era. It turns on whether the sampled cases carried concomitant Alzheimer-type cortical pathology and on which regions were taken. Because the Whitford cohort includes cognitively impaired parkinsonian cases, its null bears directly on the Beal result rather than describing a different population. Resolution status: partially resolved — the co-pathology explanation is consistent with both datasets, but no cohort stratified on Alzheimer-type pathology was retrieved by these searches.
:::

## Cerebrospinal fluid somatostatin: a direction that will not stabilise

The fluid literature does not converge, and the way it fails to converge is informative. Three cohorts report reduced somatostatin-like immunoreactivity in parkinsonian patients — lumbar cerebrospinal fluid at 19.8 +/- 9.0 fmol/ml against 32.5 +/- 12.0 fmol/ml in senile controls {cite:p}`Cramer2008`, CSF at 21.4 +/- 8.1 fmol ml-1 in 38 aged parkinsonian patients against 29.5 +/- 9.4 fmol ml-1 in senile controls {cite:p}`Strittmatter1992`, and lateral ventricular fluid at 42.9 +/- 2.9 fmol/ml against 65.3 +/- 9.7 fmol/ml in benign essential tremor patients {cite:p}`Cramer1985a` — and a fourth reports cerebroventricular SLI significantly lower in Parkinson's disease than in neurological controls with chronic stable multiple sclerosis or temporal lobe epilepsy {cite:p}`Jost1990`. Against these, {cite:t}`Espino1995` measured CSF-SLI in twenty-three untreated patients with parkinsonian syndrome at diagnosis, 15 of them with idiopathic Parkinson's disease, and found it significantly *higher* than in 26 subjects without neurological symptoms (107.9 +/- 9.8 pg/ml versus 73.5 +/- 8.4 pg/ml). That elevation remains unreplicated three decades after publication and is the single result that prevents the fluid literature from being read as a monotonic decline. Laying the cohorts side by side in {numref}`fig-sec5-csf-somatostatin-parkinson-cohorts` shows that the direction of change co-varies with fluid compartment, disease stage, comparator group and assay units rather than with a consistent biomarker signal; because units differ and somatostatin-like immunoreactivity is a mixed peptide pool, only within-study contrasts are interpretable, and no conversion between the fmol/ml and pg/ml cohorts is defensible.

:::{admonition} Evidence Conflict
:class: warning
Two disagreements sit inside this literature. First, {cite:t}`Jolkkonen1986` found CSF SLI lower than controls in 36 patients with early Parkinson's disease and 19 controls (p < 0.01), with the lowest values in demented parkinsonian patients, while {cite:t}`Volicer1986` found SLI similar in parkinsonian patients and controls. Both are contemporaneous radioimmunoassay series, so assay modality is held constant and the residual explanations are cohort cognitive status and disease stage; status: open. Second, {cite:t}`Espino1995` report elevation in untreated early parkinsonian syndrome while {cite:t}`Masson1990`, studying 35 aged patients (mean 79.5 y) against 11 controls (mean 82.3 y), report SLI lower than controls (P < .02) and *lowest in the untreated group*. Disease duration, control-group composition and the reported unit all differ, so assay calibration cannot be excluded; status: unresolved. A third, softer tension — CSF somatostatin rising early while cortical tissue somatostatin falls late {cite:p}`Kasprzak2026, Beal1990` — may not be a contradiction at all if fluid peptide reflects release from degenerating tissue rather than tissue stores, but no longitudinal within-subject CSF series spanning untreated parkinsonism to dementia was retrieved by these searches, so the two cross-sectional patterns cannot be reconciled from the available record.
:::

One widely repeated claim deserves explicit correction. CSF somatostatin is often described as a marker of cognitive decline in Parkinson's disease, yet the cohorts that tested the association directly do not support it: {cite:t}`Strittmatter1992` found SLI correlated with motor disease progression and *not* with dementia scores, in explicit contrast to Alzheimer-type dementia, and {cite:t}`Masson1990` found only non-significant trends toward lower values with greater motor severity and lower Mini-Mental State scores. {cite:t}`Jolkkonen1986` reported the lowest values in demented patients, but as a subgroup observation within a cross-sectional design. No study linking baseline CSF somatostatin to incident PDD was retrieved, and {cite:t}`Thakur2025` identify somatostatin as among the least investigated neuropeptide biomarker candidates for the diagnosis of Parkinson's disease generally, without addressing Parkinson's disease dementia. The biomarker argument is taken up in {ref}`sec-human-evidence`; here the point is that a frequently asserted association rests on data that, where tested, point the other way.

## The striatal somatostatin/NPY/NOS system under dopamine depletion

In the striatum the question changes from how much peptide remains to what the cells are doing, and the answer separates structural from functional claims. The reported effect of dopaminergic denervation on striatal somatostatin is null in both series retrieved here, neither of which states a group size, so the nulls cannot be separated from low statistical power: {cite:t}`Salin1990` found the number and topographical distribution of somatostatin-immunoreactive perikarya unaltered by unilateral 6-hydroxydopamine (6-OHDA) lesion or by alpha-methylparatyrosine-induced depletion, with no change in striatal somatostatin concentration by radioimmunoassay, and {cite:t}`FlintBeal1983` found no effect of nigral 6-hydroxydopamine or ibotenic acid lesions or pallidal hemisection on striatal SLI while intrastriatal kainic acid depleted it by 60% ipsilaterally and 25% contralaterally — locating about half of striatal SLI to intrinsic neurons. What does move the peptide is pharmacology and intrinsic damage rather than denervation, as {numref}`fig-sec5-striatal-somatostatin-by-manipulation-class` sets out. In 6-OHDA-lesioned rats, {cite:t}`Engber1992` showed that prolonged dopamine-receptor stimulation drives striatal somatostatin in both directions depending on receptor subtype and dosing regimen: intermittent D1 agonist SKF 38393 reduced somatostatin by 61% against lesioned vehicle-treated controls, continuous D2 agonist quinpirole raised it by 43%, intermittent quinpirole lowered it by 35%, and continuous SKF 38393 did not alter it. Neuropeptide Y, the co-expressed marker of the same interneuron class, behaves as a more reactive readout, rising after 6-OHDA {cite:p}`Lei2016, Maeda1993` and after MPTP {cite:p}`Obuchowicz2003`, while {cite:t}`Nitsch1993` found NPY-immunoreactive striatal neuron counts falling contralateral to a nigrostriatal lesion, in a manner dependent on the state of the striato-nigro-thalamic loop rather than on denervation alone.

The human striatal data run the other way from the rodent nulls and are thinner than their frequent citation suggests. {cite:t}`Eve1997` reported a selective increase in somatostatin mRNA in the parkinsonian basal ganglia, and {cite:t}`Cannizzaro2003` found more NPY mRNA-expressing cells with higher per-cell signal in PD striatum; {cite:t}`Chesselet2007`, by contrast, summarises the rodent literature as showing striatal somatostatin mRNA decreasing after dopaminergic lesions and increasing after cortical ischaemic lesions, a direction conflict that remains contested. {cite:t}`Kasprzak2026` relays a large reduction in striatal pre-prosomatostatin messenger RNA in aged rats, but the primary study is not identified in the retrieved text and the observation is one of ageing rather than parkinsonism. The only quantitative human counts come from two small series that disagree, and modern unbiased stereology of SST-, NPY- and nitric-oxide-synthase-expressing striatal interneurons in human Parkinson's disease using cell-type-specific markers rather than NADPH-diaphorase histochemistry was not retrieved by these searches. Every mechanistic model that assumes these interneurons survive and change function therefore rests, on the human side, on two contradictory five-case series.

:::{admonition} Evidence Conflict
:class: warning
{cite:t}`Bockelmann1994` quantified NADPH-diaphorase histochemistry in post-mortem putamen and reported control 1,120 +/- 69 per mm2 (n = 5) versus PD 575 +/- 164mm2 (n = 5) — a claim of roughly halved density that has not been replicated in the thirty-two years since publication. {cite:t}`Mufson1994` reported that striatal NADPH-diaphorase-containing neurons are spared in Parkinson's and Alzheimer's disease, with shrunken or bulbous somata and foreshortened dendrites but a significant cell-area reduction only in Alzheimer putamen. Both use the same histochemistry on small post-mortem series, so marker choice does not explain the difference; what differs is the counting denominator — a ratio of stained neurons to total cells versus absolute counts — and case selection. The source wording of the first study is itself internally inconsistent, describing a ratio while printing per-mm2 units, and that discrepancy is reproduced rather than repaired in {numref}`fig-sec5-striatal-sst-system-under-dopamine-loss`. Resolution status: unresolved. A second disagreement is about direction: rodent striatal peptide content is unchanged after denervation {cite:p}`Salin1990` while human basal ganglia show a selective increase in somatostatin transcript {cite:p}`Eve1997`. Species, modality (peptide content versus mRNA) and chronicity all differ; status: unresolved.
:::

Function, by contrast, is reported to change sharply, on the evidence of one laboratory. {cite:t}`LopezHuerta2012` recorded antidromically evoked inhibitory postsynaptic currents between spiny projection neurons in rat neostriatal slices and found that exogenous somatostatin presynaptically reduced these currents by ~34% in all control synapses tested; after dopamine deprivation, presynaptic inhibition grew to a 57% reduction in ~53% of evoked currents recorded from direct-pathway neurons, while somatostatin produced an 82% enhancement in ~43% of currents recorded from indirect-pathway neurons. A peptide that uniformly dampens feedback inhibition in the intact striatum thus acquires opposite signs on the two output pathways after denervation — a single-laboratory result, unreplicated in fourteen years, whose implications for the direct/indirect imbalance are large enough that replication matters. Converging evidence that somatostatinergic signalling gains influence after denervation comes from microdialysis, where somatostatin-evoked striatal acetylcholine release is roughly twice as effective after 6-OHDA lesion {cite:p}`Rakovska2003`. Behaviourally, intrastriatal somatostatin dose-dependently inhibits apomorphine-induced rotation in lesioned rats and the effect is blocked by cyclo-somatostatin {cite:p}`Lu2002`, though that study tested lesioned animals only and so does not itself compare the intact and denervated striatum. Blockade of brain somatostatin signalling produces catalepsy in aged but not young rats, reversed by octreotide {cite:p}`Ionov2012`. Around this peptidergic change the wider interneuron network is also reorganised: GABAergic drive onto NPY-expressing interneurons increases with giant and bursting spontaneous currents after dopamine depletion in a result reported by one laboratory {cite:p}`Rubi2020`; perisomatic parvalbumin connectivity onto projection neurons is differentially remodelled {cite:p}`Gomez2019`; interneuron-to-projection-neuron connectivity switches, including cholinergic innervation of striatopallidal neurons {cite:p}`Salin2009`; cortical input and GABAergic interneurons imbalance projection-neuron discharge independently of dopamine {cite:p}`Mallet2006`; medium calretinin interneuron density falls from 370 +/- 41 to 158 +/- 15 neurons/mm3 while small calretinin cells are unchanged {cite:p}`Petryszyn2021`; tyrosine hydroxylase interneurons shift their firing profile {cite:p}`Unal2013`; parvalbumin expression intensity falls rapidly during the second week after lesion {cite:p}`He2024`; and striatal acetylcholine is re-set relative to dopamine through D1-type receptors on cholinergic interneurons {cite:p}`McKinley2019`. That suppressing a striatal interneuron class alone suffices to generate abnormal movement is shown, for fast-spiking interneurons rather than for the somatostatin class, by selective pharmacological silencing that elicits robust dystonia-like impairments {cite:p}`Gittis2011` — one study, whose replication is not established here.

(sec-05-alpha-synuclein-burden)=

## Alpha-synuclein and interneurons: little inclusion burden, altered function

If somatostatin interneurons are affected in Lewy body disease, the mechanism is unlikely to be inclusion pathology inside those cells. Three preparations bear on how much alpha-synuclein inhibitory interneurons contain, each with a different denominator: the somatostatinergic share of pathology-bearing cells in olfactory bulb, the share of hippocampal parvalbumin interneurons bearing alpha-synuclein, and baseline expression in cultured GAD-positive neurons. In human olfactory bulb, around 8% of cells containing Lewy pathology are somatostatinergic, as against more than 50% of calcium-binding-protein-expressing cells — a contrast relayed by {cite:t}`SaizSanchez2020` and credited in its own sentence to a primary study that could not be consulted. In DLB hippocampus, {cite:t}`Bernstein2011` found that only 2% of parvalbumin-expressing interneurons were laden with alpha-synuclein immunoreactive material, yet parvalbumin-immunoreactive neuron density was significantly decreased in dentate/hilus and CA1 with total neuron density preserved. In primary mouse hippocampal culture, {cite:t}`Taguchi2014` quantified that 89±1.4% of GAD-positive neurons displayed no expression of alpha-synuclein (6 coverslips were analyzed), and found that those neurons did not form Lewy-body-like aggregates after preformed-fibril treatment unless human alpha-synuclein was exogenously expressed. The denominators of these three numbers are different in kind, which {numref}`fig-sec5-alpha-synuclein-interneuron-denominators` makes explicit rather than smoothing. The inference the data support is narrow but important: low inclusion burden coexisting with reduced marker density points to a non-cell-autonomous mechanism — network, trophic or input-driven — rather than to direct aggregate toxicity within the interneuron. It is also where the evidence stops. Systematic quantification of Lewy pathology inside identified somatostatin interneurons across cortical regions and Braak stages was not retrieved by these searches, and the available counts are for parvalbumin cells and for olfactory bulb, not for cortical or striatal somatostatin populations.

:::{admonition} Evidence Conflict
:class: warning
Whether reduced interneuron marker density means fewer interneurons is unresolved and recurs throughout this review. {cite:t}`Bernstein2011` read decreased hippocampal parvalbumin density in DLB as partial interneuron loss; {cite:t}`Schreiber2024` argue from a hamster infection model with alpha-synuclein dysregulation that increases or decreases in parvalbumin- or calretinin-positive counts are unlikely to represent loss or gain of neurons and more likely reflect up- or down-regulation of the calcium-binding protein used to identify them. The same ambiguity has a directional variant: {cite:t}`Radovanovic2021` found an *increased* number of dentate parvalbumin interneurons in rats with cholinergic pedunculopontine lesions, opposite to the human DLB finding, and a reduced dentate parvalbumin number when the nigral and pedunculopontine lesions are combined, so the direction turns on which systems are lesioned; the coupling to sleep-wake architecture belongs to the combined-lesion condition alone, where the reduction in parvalbumin number tracked shorter hippocampal wake episodes (r = 0.52, p = 0.01) and no such relation held in the other groups. {cite:t}`Hawksworth2026` add a regional qualifier, reporting lower parvalbumin interneuron density in DLB cingulate gyrus (t = 2.838, df = 25.86, p = 0.0087) but not inferior temporal gyrus (p = 0.1370), with MAP2-positive neuronal density preserved. None of these studies identifies cells independently of the marker, which is exactly the methodological gap that {ref}`sec-conflicts-limitations` treats as a review-wide confound.
:::

Model systems add a mixed picture rather than a clean one. Alpha-synuclein overexpression does damage interneurons in some preparations — progressive loss of striatal parvalbumin GABAergic interneurons in SNCA transgenic rats {cite:p}`Paldino2022`, and up to a 50% reduction of cholinergic interneurons in striatum and cortex after AAV-mediated forebrain overexpression {cite:p}`AldrinKirk2014` — while in transgenic mouse hippocampus interneuron marker expression was generally unchanged across ages, differing only for calbindin and somatostatin at 43 weeks {cite:p}`FloresCuadrado2016`. Organotypic slice seeding reproduces templated, serine-129-phosphorylated aggregation that spreads between connected hippocampal regions and offers a preparation in which interneuronal transfer can be dissected {cite:p}`Elfarrash2019`. In human anterior olfactory nucleus, somatostatin immunoreactivity in neurites is reduced while parvalbumin is elevated {cite:p}`UbedaBanon2017` — the same cell-type ordering as the olfactory-bulb pathology-burden shares, although marker level and inclusion burden are different quantities measured in different series. One genetic model suggests a cell-autonomous route after all: GABAergic neurons differentiated from PARK2 patient iPSCs showed significantly reduced SST transcript and fewer somatostatin-positive neurons {cite:p}`Iwasawa2019` — a single-study observation, unreplicated in seven years, in a monogenic form of the disease. No somatostatin receptor subtype quantification in human parkinsonian or Lewy body tissue was retrieved by these searches, so therapeutic proposals aimed at particular SSTR subtypes in synucleinopathy, discussed in {ref}`sec-therapeutics`, currently rest on receptor distributions not measured in the disease tissue in anything these searches returned; the neuroprotective properties attributed to the peptide come from other preparations, including suppression of nigral microglial inflammatory mediators {cite:p}`BAI2015`, Y2-receptor-mediated protection by the co-expressed neuropeptide Y {cite:p}`Decressac2012`, and the general neuropeptide-protection literature summarised by {cite:t}`Behl2022`.

## L-DOPA, dyskinesia and cognitive decline

Treatment is both a confound and a phenomenon, and cohorts that mix the two cannot be compared. Chronic L-DOPA alters the peptidergic and nitrergic readouts directly: it raises whole-brain somatostatin while lowering neuropeptide Y in cortex, hippocampus and striatum {cite:p}`Maeda1993`, normalises the late striatal somatostatin deficit seen after MPTP while itself lowering cortical somatostatin {cite:p}`Asanuma1990`, and in the dyskinetic state raises striatal neuronal nitric oxide synthase (nNOS) protein between 19 and 24% with a ten-times larger increase in nNOS mRNA in the striatum contralateral to the lesion, while nitric oxide synthase inhibition reduces abnormal involuntary movements {cite:p}`PadovanNeto2011`. Because nNOS is expressed by the somatostatin/NPY interneuron class, as set out in {ref}`sec-sst-identity`, and by other sources, that therapeutic effect does not identify its causal cell type, and the evidence gap recorded for this literature — no cell-type-specific manipulation isolating striatal somatostatin interneurons during dyskinesia — has only just begun to close: {cite:t}`Stahl2026` report increased c-Fos in striatal somatostatin interneurons during L-DOPA-induced dyskinesia and show that chemogenetic inhibition modestly but consistently exacerbates dyskinesia selectively during the wearing-off phase, without altering the antiparkinsonian response. That is one abstract-level study, not a replicated finding. Around it, the dyskinetic striatum shows cholinergic interneurons whose calcium events the authors report as about 50% larger, which they read as the in vivo counterpart of a bursting-like phenotype seen in slices {cite:p}`Atamna2025`, perineuronal-net remodelling around parvalbumin interneurons in dorsolateral striatum and M1 {cite:p}`Bilbay2025`, and dopamine-receptor-subtype-dependent swings in peptide content {cite:p}`Engber1992`. That some of this remodelling is dopamine-dependent and pharmacologically reversible is shown in hippocampus, where midbrain dopamine loss destabilises perineuronal nets and tissue plasminogen activator around parvalbumin interneurons and D2/D3 receptor activation restores net integrity and inhibitory drive {cite:p}`Cauzzi2026`. Medication also moves peripheral measures: serum somatostatin rises significantly after starting antiparkinson therapy in patients who experience nausea or vomiting {cite:p}`Shiraishi2010`, so treated and de novo cohorts differ at least in peripheral peptide; whether medication shifts cerebrospinal fluid somatostatin was not measured in any series retrieved here.

The cognitive end of the syndrome is where the cortical and striatal stories meet, and the interneuron evidence there is thinner than the peptide evidence. Plasma neuropeptide Y is reported lower in Parkinson's disease than in healthy controls and to decline stepwise with cognitive impairment, lowest in PDD {cite:p}`Zhang2026b` — a single recent cohort, unreplicated — while CSF neuropeptide Y is higher in depressed parkinsonian patients than in major depressive disorder {cite:p}`Svenningsson2017`, indicating that peripheral and central peptide measures are not reading the same variable. Functional cortical evidence points to somatostatin interneurons as participants rather than casualties: somatostatin interneuron somatic activity in motor cortex falls within an hour of MPTP and driving those interneurons rescues spine loss and motor deficits {cite:p}`Chen2019`, subthalamic deep brain stimulation activates motor-cortex somatostatin interneurons while inhibiting parvalbumin interneurons and pyramidal cells, with direct optogenetic activation of the somatostatin population alone relieving motor symptoms {cite:p}`Valverde2020`, and cortical inhibitory dynamics and their neuromodulation are altered after dopamine loss in rodents {cite:p}`Minetti2025, Gui2010` and in people assessed with directionally selective transcranial magnetic stimulation {cite:p}`OFarrell2026`. Parkinson-spectrum specificity is suggested by the finding that somatostatin concentrations were normal in every region tested in progressive supranuclear palsy despite reduced choline acetyltransferase {cite:p}`Epelbaum1987`, and by the nigral somatostatin *increase*, not decrease, seen after MPTP in mice {cite:p}`Radke1987`; lesioning also shifts the biotransformation of somatostatin and other precursors rather than simply depleting them {cite:p}`Nilsson2009`. Two further gaps should be stated plainly: no head-to-head quantification of cortical and CSF somatostatin in DLB and PDD within a single cohort and one assay was retrieved, so the two syndromes have no biochemical separator in this record; and no cortical or striatal somatostatin-specific inclusion count was retrieved by these searches, so the non-cell-autonomous inference above is untested here.

These conclusions are bounded by how the literature was assembled. Retrieval was dominated by one biomedical full-text index with no citation chasing, so no claim of complete coverage is made and statements of absence are statements about what these searches returned. Only a minority of the parkinsonian literature here was available as full text — the 1980s and 1990s radioimmunoassay and cerebrospinal fluid series are recorded at abstract level — which is why this section quotes source sentences and declines to recompute percentages, convert units or pool values. Searches for disconfirming results were capped, so the reported nulls are a top-relevance sample rather than a census and the rate of contradiction is bounded below, not estimated.

The parkinsonian picture that survives these constraints is of a peptidergic system responding to an altered network state rather than dying: cortical somatostatin falls where Alzheimer-type pathology and dementia are present and not otherwise, striatal somatostatin is unmoved by denervation itself while its synaptic action is reconfigured, and, where inclusion burden has been counted at all — olfactory bulb somatostatinergic cells and hippocampal parvalbumin cells — the cells carry few inclusions. The cross-disease direction-of-change contrast set out in {ref}`sec-selective-vulnerability` is instantiated here in its sharpest form: the same cell class reads as deficient in cortex, as upregulated in basal ganglia transcript, and as unchanged in rodent striatal peptide, depending entirely on what was measured. {ref}`sec-huntingtons` presents the extreme version of that idea — a striatal interneuron population long described as spared while its projection-neuron targets are destroyed — and asks whether the sparing claim survives modern measurement.
